### Which of the following is an extended coverage peril?

- [x] Windstorm
- [ ] Falling objects
- [ ] Freezing of plumbing
- [ ] Weight of ice, snow or sleet

**Extended coverage is the second tier, not the third.** It adds
**windstorm, hail, explosion, riot or civil commotion, aircraft, vehicles,
smoke and volcanic eruption** to a DP-1's three basic perils.

**All three wrong answers are BROAD perils** — they arrive with the DP-2, not
with extended coverage, and that is the whole point of the question.

The tiers, which are worth holding as a sequence rather than three lists:

1. **DP-1 unendorsed** — fire, lightning, internal explosion. Three perils.
2. **Plus extended coverage** — the eight above. Still DP-1.
3. **DP-2 broad** — everything above plus falling objects; weight of ice, snow
   or sleet; accidental discharge of water or steam; tearing apart of a
   heating system; freezing; accidental damage from artificially generated
   electrical current; and damage by burglars.

**A stem naming freezing, falling objects or the weight of snow is describing
broad coverage**, whatever else it says.

### A DP-2 insured is burgled: the front door is forced and a television is taken. What does the broad form pay for?

- [x] The door, under damage by burglars — not the television
- [ ] Both, since damage by burglars covers the whole incident
- [ ] The television only; the door is wear and tear
- [ ] Neither; burglary is excluded from all dwelling forms

**Damage by burglars pays for what burglars BREAK, not what they TAKE.** The
forced door is covered; the stolen television is not.

**Theft of contents needs theft coverage**, which a dwelling form adds by
endorsement rather than including in the broad peril list.

It is the same boundary as commercial property, where Basic and Broad reach
vandalism and **only Special adds theft** — the forms are consistent about
this, which makes it worth learning once.

### A landlord owns a rented house and wants building coverage plus protection against a tenant's injury claim. What is needed?

- [x] A dwelling policy with a personal liability endorsement
- [ ] A dwelling policy alone, which includes liability
- [ ] An HO-3
- [ ] An HO-4 in the landlord's name

**A dwelling policy is property insurance alone.** Liability and medical
payments are added by endorsement — precisely what a landlord needs, since an
injured tenant will look to the owner.

### Under a DP-1, a covered fire destroys a 30-year-old roof. How is the loss settled?

- [x] Actual cash value — replacement cost less depreciation
- [ ] Replacement cost, with the holdback paid on completion
- [ ] Agreed value
- [ ] Functional replacement cost using common materials

**DP-1 offers no replacement cost option.** DP-2 and DP-3 settle the dwelling at
replacement cost, subject to the usual insurance-to-value requirement.

### Which dwelling form matches the HO-3's peril structure?

- [x] DP-3 — open peril dwelling, named peril contents
- [ ] DP-1
- [ ] DP-2
- [ ] None; no dwelling form writes open peril

The same asymmetry, tested the same way: **open peril on the dwelling and other
structures, named peril on personal property**.

### A house stands empty with all furniture removed for 75 days. Vandals break in and damage it. Likely outcome?

- [x] No coverage — the dwelling is vacant beyond 60 days and vandalism is suspended
- [ ] Covered in full; vacancy affects only theft
- [ ] Covered, reduced by 25 percent
- [ ] Covered only if the insured reported the vacancy after the loss

**Vacant** means **both the people and the contents are gone**. After a stated
period, commonly 60 consecutive days, vandalism, glass breakage, theft,
sprinkler leakage and water damage are typically suspended.

### A family leaves for a three-month trip, furniture still in place. Is the dwelling vacant or unoccupied?

- [x] Unoccupied — the contents remain
- [ ] Vacant, because nobody is living there
- [ ] Neither, because the absence is temporary
- [ ] Both terms apply equally

**Unoccupied**: nobody living there, contents remain. **Vacant**: people and
contents both gone. **Vacancy is the one that restricts coverage.**

### Is theft included in a standard DP-2?

- [x] No — it is added by endorsement on DP-2 and DP-3
- [ ] Yes, on the same terms as a homeowners policy
- [ ] Yes, but only for building items
- [ ] No, and it cannot be added to any dwelling form

**Theft is not standard on any dwelling form**, and is generally not available at
all on DP-1.

### How does a dwelling policy handle loss of use differently from a homeowners policy?

- [x] It splits fair rental value and additional living expense into separate lettered coverages D and E
- [ ] It omits loss of use entirely
- [ ] It pays loss of use only to tenants
- [ ] It combines loss of use with Coverage B

Homeowners combines both into Coverage D. The dwelling form separates them — and
**DP-1 does not include Coverage E at all**.

### What three perils does an unendorsed DP-1 cover?

- [x] Fire, lightning and internal explosion
- [ ] Fire, windstorm and theft
- [ ] Fire, lightning and vandalism
- [ ] Fire, lightning and water damage

Extended coverage — windstorm, hail, explosion, riot, aircraft, vehicles, smoke,
volcanic eruption — is added on top, and **vandalism and malicious mischief**
above that.

### Which dwelling is eligible for a dwelling policy but not a homeowners policy?

- [x] A non-owner-occupied rental house
- [ ] A four-family dwelling with the owner in one unit
- [ ] A condominium unit the owner lives in
- [ ] A single-family home the owner occupies

Dwelling policies exist for houses **not owner-occupied**, seasonal or vacant, of
too low a value or too poor a condition for a homeowners policy, or where the
owner wants building coverage only.

### A landlord's dwelling has been vacant between tenants and the landlord expects it to stay empty for several months. What is the correct step?

- [x] Tell the insurer and buy a vacancy permit
- [ ] Nothing; the vacancy provision applies only to owner-occupied dwellings
- [ ] Cancel the policy and rewrite it at the next tenancy
- [ ] Increase the Coverage C limit to offset the restriction

An empty building is a magnet for vandalism and a slow burn nobody notices. The
insured's remedy is to disclose the vacancy and buy the permit rather than
discover the suspension at claim time.
