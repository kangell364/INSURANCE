### A homeowner buys an NFIP policy on 1 May with no lender requirement. The river floods on 20 May. Is there coverage?

- [x] No — the 30-day waiting period has not run
- [ ] Yes, coverage attaches on payment of the premium
- [ ] Yes, at half the policy limit
- [ ] Only for contents, not the building

The waiting period is the programme's defence against **buying cover as the
river rises**. The exceptions: no wait where a lender requires the coverage in
connection with a loan; a one-day wait after a flood map change; and a shortened
wait for property affected by wildfire on federal land.

### A burst water main floods only the insured's basement. Is this a "flood" under the programme?

- [x] No — a flood requires inundation of two or more acres or two or more properties
- [ ] Yes, any accumulation of surface water is a flood
- [ ] Yes, because the water originated outside the building
- [ ] Only if the main was municipally owned

The definition is **general and temporary inundation of two or more acres of
normally dry land, or of two or more properties**. Water entering one building
alone — a burst pipe, a backed-up sewer, a leaking roof — is not a flood.

### An owner buys $250,000 of NFIP building coverage. A flood destroys $60,000 of furniture. What is paid?

- [x] Nothing — contents coverage must be purchased separately
- [ ] $60,000, within the building limit
- [ ] $100,000, the residential contents maximum
- [ ] $25,000, a percentage of the building limit

**Contents are not automatically included.** An owner who buys building coverage
only has no coverage for anything inside, and this is a favourite question.

### A rental house owned as an investment suffers flood damage to a 15-year-old roof. Replacement cost or actual cash value?

- [x] Actual cash value
- [ ] Replacement cost, since the building is insured to value
- [ ] Replacement cost, reduced by the deductible
- [ ] Functional replacement cost

**Replacement cost is limited to a single-family dwelling that is the insured's
principal residence**, insured to at least 80% of replacement cost. Everything
else — contents always, and buildings that are secondary homes, rentals or
commercial — settles at ACV.

### A community declines to adopt flood plain management ordinances. Can its residents buy NFIP coverage?

- [x] No — community participation is a precondition
- [ ] Yes, at a surcharged rate
- [ ] Yes, but only under the emergency programme
- [ ] Yes, if the property is outside a Special Flood Hazard Area

**The NFIP is a bargain with the community, not just the individual.** The
community adopts and enforces building and development standards; in exchange,
federally backed coverage becomes available to owners within it.

### What is the maximum NFIP building coverage on a commercial property?

- [x] $500,000, with a further $500,000 available for contents
- [ ] $250,000, with $100,000 for contents
- [ ] $1,000,000 combined
- [ ] There is no maximum under the regular programme

Residential maximums are **$250,000 building and $100,000 contents**. These are
**programme maximums, not appraised values** — anybody needing more buys **excess
flood** from the private market.

### Why did Congress have to create the NFIP in the first place?

- [x] Flood losses are not independent, and adverse selection is extreme
- [ ] Private insurers were prohibited by statute from writing flood
- [ ] Flood losses are too small to interest private insurers
- [ ] Flood risk cannot be measured or mapped

A flood does not damage one house; it damages **every house on the flood plain at
once**. The law of large numbers needs many unrelated exposures, and only people
who expect to flood buy the coverage.

### Which of these is covered by an NFIP policy?

- [x] A permanently installed water heater
- [ ] A finished basement's carpet and furniture
- [ ] Additional living expense while the home is uninhabitable
- [ ] Landscaping, a deck and a swimming pool

**Contents in a basement** are largely excluded beyond a short list of building
items. The programme also excludes loss of use, currency and valuable papers,
property outside the building, vehicles, and business interruption.

### What do Zones A and V designate on a Flood Insurance Rate Map?

- [x] Special Flood Hazard Areas — the 100-year flood plain, with V zones coastal
- [ ] Moderate and minimal risk areas
- [ ] Areas where NFIP coverage is unavailable
- [ ] Areas exempt from the mandatory purchase requirement

A Special Flood Hazard Area carries a **1% annual chance of flooding**; **V zones
are coastal**, with additional wave action. **Zones B, C and X** are moderate or
minimal risk, where coverage is cheaper and often bought voluntarily.

### What triggers the NFIP's mandatory purchase requirement?

- [x] A building in a Special Flood Hazard Area with a federally backed or federally regulated mortgage
- [ ] Any building in a participating community
- [ ] Any building within one mile of a river or coast
- [ ] A prior flood claim on the property

The mandatory purchase rule is **the single largest driver of NFIP sales**.

### Under the Write Your Own programme, who carries the flood risk?

- [x] The federal government; the private insurer issues and services the policy
- [ ] The private insurer, with federal reinsurance above a retention
- [ ] The participating community
- [ ] The insurer and the government share it equally

The producer sells what looks like a private policy, but **the terms, limits and
rates are the programme's** and the risk is federal.

### Mud flows down a hillside into the insured's house. Is it covered?

- [x] Yes — mudflow is within the flood definition
- [ ] No, all earth movement is excluded
- [ ] Only if the house is in a V zone
- [ ] Only under the emergency programme

**Mudflow counts; landslide generally does not.** Mudflow is liquid mud flowing;
a landslide is earth movement, which is excluded.
