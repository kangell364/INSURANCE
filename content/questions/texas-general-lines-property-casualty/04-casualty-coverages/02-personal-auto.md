### Under 250/500/100, three people are injured with bodily injury claims of $200,000, $200,000 and $150,000. What is paid for bodily injury?

- [x] $500,000
- [ ] $550,000
- [ ] $750,000
- [ ] $250,000

Each claim is within the **$250,000 per-person** limit, but the $550,000 total
exceeds the **$500,000 per-accident** limit. Split limits read **per person /
per accident / property damage per accident**.

### The insured hits a deer. Collision or other than collision?

- [x] Other than collision
- [ ] Collision
- [ ] Neither; animal strikes are excluded
- [ ] Specified perils only

**Contact with a bird or animal is a comprehensive loss.** The classic
counterpart: **swerving to avoid a deer and hitting a tree is collision.**

### The insured is at fault in an accident and is badly injured. The other driver is uninsured. Does UM pay the insured's injuries?

- [x] No — UM requires the insured to be legally entitled to recover from the other driver
- [ ] Yes, UM is first-party no-fault coverage
- [ ] Yes, reduced by the insured's percentage of fault
- [ ] Yes, but only up to the medical payments limit

**UM is not no-fault coverage.** It pays what the other driver's liability policy
would have paid had they carried one — so the other driver must be at fault. If
the insured caused the accident, UM pays nothing.

### An at-fault driver carries $30,000 of bodily injury coverage. The insured's damages are $90,000 and their UIM limit is $100,000. Roughly what does UIM pay?

- [x] $60,000
- [ ] $90,000
- [ ] $100,000
- [ ] $30,000

**UIM pays the difference**, not the whole loss again — the shortfall between the
at-fault driver's limit and the insured's damages, within the UIM limit.

### A friend borrows the insured's car and causes an accident. Whose policy is primary?

- [x] The car owner's — the permissive user is an insured
- [ ] The friend's own policy
- [ ] Whichever policy has the higher limit
- [ ] Both, by equal shares

**Liability follows the car.** Part A insures the named insured and family
members for any auto, **any person using the covered auto with permission**, and
anyone legally responsible for an insured's use of it.

### The insured's car is totalled. It cost $38,000 last year, is now worth $26,000, and the loan balance is $31,000. What does Part D pay?

- [x] $26,000 less the deductible — actual cash value
- [ ] $31,000, the loan balance
- [ ] $38,000, the purchase price
- [ ] $26,000 with no deductible on a total loss

Loss settlement is **ACV or the cost of repair, whichever is less**. The $5,000
shortfall to the loan is the insured's, unless **GAP coverage** was bought — which
is exactly the gap GAP exists to fill.

### Which of these does uninsured motorist coverage NOT respond to?

- [x] An at-fault driver who has insurance but not enough to cover the loss
- [ ] A driver with no insurance at all
- [ ] A hit-and-run driver who cannot be identified
- [ ] A driver whose insurer is insolvent

That first situation is **underinsured** motorist coverage. UM reaches the
uninsured, the unidentified hit-and-run driver, and the driver whose insurer is
insolvent or denies coverage.

### An insured drives a company car daily and it is not listed on the personal auto policy. Is it covered?

- [x] No — a vehicle available for regular use that is not a covered auto is excluded
- [ ] Yes, as a non-owned auto
- [ ] Yes, as a temporary substitute auto
- [ ] Yes, for liability but not physical damage

The **regular use** exclusion is precisely why a daily company car falls outside a
personal policy. A **temporary substitute** — used while a covered auto is out of
service for breakdown, repair, servicing, loss or destruction — is covered
automatically.

### An insured begins driving for a ride-hailing service. What does the unendorsed policy do?

- [x] Excludes the exposure as a public or livery conveyance
- [ ] Covers it as ordinary business use of a private passenger auto
- [ ] Covers it only while no passenger is in the car
- [ ] Covers liability but excludes physical damage

The **public or livery conveyance** exclusion is the ride-hailing exclusion. It is
now commonly bought back by endorsement, and the driver who assumes it is covered
without one has an expensive surprise coming.

### Who does Part B medical payments cover?

- [x] The named insured and family members in any auto or struck as pedestrians, plus any other occupant of the covered auto
- [ ] Only the named insured, and only in the covered auto
- [ ] Only passengers, not the driver
- [ ] Anyone injured in the accident, including the other driver

It pays **reasonable and necessary medical and funeral expenses regardless of
fault**, within a stated period — commonly three years — and it pays quickly
without waiting for a liability determination.

### What does a $300,000 combined single limit allow that 100/300/50 does not?

- [x] Bodily injury and property damage in any proportion up to the single figure
- [ ] Unlimited property damage coverage
- [ ] A separate limit for each injured person
- [ ] Defence costs inside the limit rather than in addition

A CSL is **one figure covering both**, so it can pay $250,000 of injury and
$50,000 of damage or any other split. It is more flexible and generally more
expensive. Under either structure, **defence costs are in addition to the limit**.

### An insured buys an additional car and has an accident before telling the insurer. Is it covered?

- [x] Yes, automatically for a limited period — commonly 14 or 30 days — with notice required within that window
- [ ] No, coverage begins only when the vehicle is added
- [ ] Yes, permanently, once the premium is adjusted
- [ ] Only for liability, never for physical damage

**An additional vehicle generally gets broader automatic coverage than a
replacement**, which inherits only the coverage the replaced vehicle had.

### Which of these is excluded under Part D?

- [x] Road damage to tyres and mechanical breakdown
- [ ] Theft of the vehicle
- [ ] Glass breakage
- [ ] Flood damage

Part D excludes **wear and tear, mechanical breakdown, road damage to tyres,
freezing, electronic equipment not permanently installed**, and custom
furnishings in a pickup or van unless declared.

### After a hit-and-run, what additional duty does the insured have?

- [x] Notify the police, usually within 24 hours, as a condition of UM coverage
- [ ] Obtain a written statement from a witness
- [ ] File a proof of loss within 60 days
- [ ] Notify the state motor vehicle department

UM also brings duties to **submit to physical examination and examination under
oath**, and to **allow inspection of the vehicle before repair**.

### What is stacking?

- [x] Combining the UM limits of several vehicles or policies, permitted in some states and prohibited in others
- [ ] Applying both collision and other than collision to one loss
- [ ] Adding UIM on top of the at-fault driver's limit
- [ ] Layering an umbrella above a personal auto policy

Stacking is a state-law question, not a policy-language one — which is why the
answer differs from state to state.
