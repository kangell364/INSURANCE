### An employee is injured by a co-worker's carelessness. Can the employer rely on the fellow servant rule?

- [x] No — the employer surrendered that defence as part of the bargain
- [ ] Yes, it remains available where the co-worker was solely at fault
- [ ] Yes, but only to reduce benefits proportionally
- [ ] Only where the employer had a written safety policy

The trade: the employee gives up the right to sue and the chance of a large
award; the employer gives up **contributory negligence, assumption of risk and
the fellow servant rule** and pays **without regard to fault**.

### An employee is hurt by a defective press. Who may be sued, and by whom?

- [x] The press manufacturer may be sued by the employee; the employer may not be, by exclusive remedy
- [ ] Both the manufacturer and the employer may be sued by the employee
- [ ] Only the employer, which then sues the manufacturer
- [ ] Neither; workers' compensation bars all litigation

**Third parties are not protected by exclusive remedy.** The employee sues the
manufacturer, and the compensation insurer subrogates against it.

### Is there a dollar limit on workers' compensation medical benefits?

- [x] No — medical benefits are generally unlimited, with no deductible
- [ ] Yes, the Part One limit shown on the declarations
- [ ] Yes, a statutory cap that varies by state
- [ ] Yes, the Part Two bodily injury by disease limit

This surprises candidates and is worth remembering. **Part One has no dollar
limit** because the obligation is the statute's, not the policy's. Disability
income, by contrast, is capped and percentage-based.

### An employer with operations in Oklahoma has only Texas in Item 3.A and nothing in 3.C. An Oklahoma employee is injured. Covered?

- [x] No — a state in neither 3.A nor 3.C has no coverage, and it cannot be added after the injury
- [ ] Yes, Part Three covers all states automatically
- [ ] Yes, if Oklahoma is added by endorsement within 30 days
- [ ] Yes, at the Texas benefit level

**Part Three, Other States Insurance**, extends Part One to states listed in Item
3.C where the insured **may** begin operations. **Monopolistic states cannot be
covered there at all** — coverage must be bought from the state fund.

### An employer's experience modification factor is 1.25. What does that mean?

- [x] Losses are 25% worse than expected for its class, so the premium is increased by 25%
- [ ] The employer receives a 25% credit for good experience
- [ ] The payroll estimate must be increased by 25%
- [ ] The employer must carry 25% more employers' liability limit

**Above 1.0 means worse than average and a higher premium; below 1.0 means better
and a credit.** It is the mechanism that rewards safety.

### An employee slips in the employer's own car park on the way home. Compensable?

- [x] Usually yes — the employer's car park is generally treated as premises
- [ ] No, the going and coming rule bars all commuting injuries
- [ ] Only if the employee was carrying work materials
- [ ] Only if the employer owned rather than leased the car park

**The commute is generally not covered** — the going and coming rule — but the
employer's own premises, and travel **on the employer's business**, sit inside
the system.

### What is the workers' compensation premium formula?

- [x] (Payroll ÷ 100) × rate × experience modification factor
- [ ] Payroll × rate ÷ experience modification factor
- [ ] (Revenue ÷ 1,000) × rate × loss ratio
- [ ] Number of employees × classification rate

Payroll is measured per **$100 of remuneration, by classification code** — a
business with clerical and roofing staff pays very different rates on each.

### Why does a workers' compensation policy end with a premium audit?

- [x] Payroll is not known in advance, so the policy is written on estimated payroll with a deposit premium
- [ ] The insurer must verify the experience modification factor annually
- [ ] State law requires an audit before benefits are paid
- [ ] The audit sets the following year's classification codes

**The insured must permit the audit and keep records** — it is a policy condition.
Larger employers may instead use **retrospective rating**, where the final premium
turns on their own losses within a maximum and minimum.

### Which part of the policy carries stated limits?

- [x] Part Two, employers' liability
- [ ] Part One, workers' compensation
- [ ] Part Three, other states insurance
- [ ] All three carry the same limit

**Part One has no dollar limit**; Part Two's limits are commonly written as three:
**bodily injury by accident each accident / bodily injury by disease policy limit
/ bodily injury by disease each employee.**

### An injured employee sues a machine manufacturer, which then sues the employer for contribution. Which coverage responds?

- [x] Part Two, employers' liability — a third-party-over action
- [ ] Part One, workers' compensation
- [ ] The commercial general liability policy
- [ ] Neither; exclusive remedy bars the claim

Part Two answers the exposures that **fall outside the compensation statute**:
third-party-over actions, consequential bodily injury to a family member, loss of
consortium, and **dual capacity** claims.

### Is FELA a no-fault system?

- [x] No — it preserves the right to sue but removes the common law defences
- [ ] Yes, on the same basis as state workers' compensation
- [ ] Yes, but only for injuries occurring on navigable waters
- [ ] No, it requires proof of intentional misconduct

**FELA** covers railroad workers. The other federal systems: **Longshore and
Harbor Workers'** for maritime workers other than seamen, **the Jones Act** for
seamen, and **FECA** for civilian federal employees.

### Which test decides whether somebody is an employee rather than an independent contractor?

- [x] Whether the employer directs and controls the manner of the work
- [ ] Whether the person is paid hourly rather than by the job
- [ ] Whether a written contract describes them as an employee
- [ ] Whether they work more than 30 hours a week

**The label on the relationship matters less than the control test.** An
independent contractor controls how the work is done, supplies their own tools
and serves multiple clients — though an employer using uninsured subcontractors
may still be treated as their statutory employer.

### An occupational hearing loss develops gradually over fifteen years of factory work. Compensable?

- [x] Yes — occupational disease is covered where it arises from conditions characteristic of the work
- [ ] No, there was no accident
- [ ] No, the onset was gradual rather than sudden
- [ ] Only if the employer failed to provide hearing protection

Compensability requires that the injury **arise out of** the employment and occur
**in the course of** it. Repetitive strain, hearing loss and exposure illnesses
qualify despite the absence of an accident.

### Which disability class is typically paid on a schedule setting a number of weeks per body part?

- [x] Permanent partial
- [ ] Temporary total
- [ ] Temporary partial
- [ ] Permanent total

**Temporary total**: cannot work at all but will recover. **Temporary partial**:
reduced capacity, will recover. **Permanent partial**: a lasting impairment, often
scheduled. **Permanent total**: cannot return to any gainful employment.
