### A contractor installs a faulty water heater. It leaks two years later, ruining a floor. Which exposure is this?

- [x] Completed operations — the work was finished and handed over
- [ ] Operations, because the contractor performed the work
- [ ] Premises, because the loss occurred at a location
- [ ] Contractual, because the work was done under a contract

**Work in progress is operations; work handed over is completed operations.**
Products and completed operations are grouped because the loss happens when the
insured is no longer there, often long afterwards — and they share their own
separate aggregate limit.

### On those facts, is the water heater itself covered?

- [x] No — damage to your product and damage to your work are excluded
- [ ] Yes, as property damage caused by an occurrence
- [ ] Yes, up to the damage to premises rented to you limit
- [ ] Yes, but only after the floor claim is settled

**The policy covers the harm the product does, not the cost of the defective
product itself.** That is the business risk principle: replacing your own bad
work is a cost of doing business, not an insurable accident. The floor is
covered; the heater is not.

### A CGL has a $2m general aggregate, exhausted by premises claims in July, and a $2m products-completed operations aggregate. A products claim arrives in September. Covered?

- [x] Yes — products-completed operations has its own separate aggregate
- [ ] No, the aggregate for the policy period is exhausted
- [ ] Yes, but only up to the remaining occurrence limit
- [ ] Only if the insured buys a reinstatement endorsement

**Exhausting one aggregate does not exhaust the other.** The aggregate is annual,
and when a relevant one is exhausted the policy is finished for that period even
though months remain — and the duty to defend ends with it.

### A visitor trips in the insured's lobby. The insured is plainly not negligent, but the visitor has a $3,000 hospital bill. Anything payable?

- [x] Yes — Coverage C medical payments pays regardless of fault
- [ ] No, liability coverage requires legal liability
- [ ] Yes, but only as a supplementary payment
- [ ] Only if the visitor signs a release

Coverage C settles small injuries quickly and goodwill intact, before they become
liability claims. Expenses must be incurred within a stated period, commonly
**three years**. It does **not** apply to the insured, employees or tenants.

### An employee is injured by a colleague's negligence. Does the CGL respond?

- [x] No — employee injuries are excluded; workers' compensation and employers' liability respond
- [ ] Yes, under Coverage A as an occurrence
- [ ] Yes, under Coverage C medical payments
- [ ] Yes, because the colleague is an insured under the form

Employees and volunteer workers are insureds for acts within the scope of
employment, **but not for bodily injury to a fellow employee**.

### A restaurant's advertisement copies a competitor's slogan. Which coverage?

- [x] Coverage B — personal and advertising injury
- [ ] Coverage A, as property damage to an intangible asset
- [ ] Coverage C
- [ ] None; intellectual property claims are excluded entirely

Coverage B's offences include **infringement of copyright, trade dress or slogan
in the insured's advertisement**. Note what is absent: **patent and trademark
infringement are generally not covered.**

### A tenant's negligence causes a fire that damages the landlord's building. Covered, given the care, custody or control exclusion?

- [x] Yes, up to the damage to premises rented to you limit
- [ ] No, the exclusion is absolute
- [ ] Yes, up to the general aggregate
- [ ] Only if the lease is an insured contract

**Damage to premises rented to you is the express exception** to the care,
custody or control exclusion. A tenant who burns the landlord's building has
this, and nothing else.

### What is an occurrence under Coverage A?

- [x] An accident, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same general harmful conditions
- [ ] Any claim first made during the policy period
- [ ] Any act or omission of the insured, intended or not
- [ ] A loss exceeding the self-insured retention

The word does the work of **excluding the deliberate**: intentional injury is not
an occurrence. Coverage B, by contrast, is triggered by **offences**, not
accidents.

### A claims-made policy's retroactive date moves forward on renewal. What is the effect?

- [x] Years of prior coverage are quietly destroyed — injury before the new date is never covered
- [ ] Nothing; the retroactive date affects only reporting deadlines
- [ ] Coverage widens, because the reporting window lengthens
- [ ] The extended reporting period becomes automatic

This is the thing to watch for on a claims-made renewal. **Injury or damage
before the retroactive date is not covered, no matter when claimed.**

### What is the difference between a basic and a supplemental extended reporting period?

- [x] Basic is automatic and short; supplemental is bought, usually unlimited in time, within a short window after the policy ends
- [ ] Basic is bought; supplemental is automatic
- [ ] Basic covers products claims; supplemental covers premises claims
- [ ] They differ only in the limit available

Tails exist because claims-made forms respond to **claims first made during the
policy period** — without a tail, an expired claims-made policy leaves the
insured exposed to claims arising from covered work.

### A forklift used solely on the insured's premises injures a visiting driver. Which policy responds?

- [x] The CGL — a forklift is mobile equipment, not an auto
- [ ] The commercial auto policy
- [ ] Workers' compensation
- [ ] Neither; the vehicle exclusion applies

**Mobile equipment** — bulldozers, farm machinery, forklifts, vehicles maintained
solely on the premises, and vehicles carrying permanently mounted equipment like
drills or cranes — **is covered by the CGL**. Vehicles subject to compulsory
registration and used on public roads are **autos**, and belong on the commercial
auto policy.

### A construction contract obliges the insured to assume the owner's tort liability. Does the contractual liability exclusion defeat coverage?

- [x] No — an insured contract is the exception, and it covers most commercial hold-harmless agreements
- [ ] Yes, all assumed liability is excluded
- [ ] Yes, unless the owner is added as an additional insured
- [ ] Only if the assumption was oral

**Insured contract** is a defined list — leases, sidetrack agreements, easements,
and the part of any contract assuming another's tort liability — broad enough to
restore most commercial hold-harmless agreements.

### The insured acquires a new subsidiary in March and does not report it. Is it an insured?

- [x] Yes, automatically for a limited period — commonly 90 days or until the policy ends, whichever is first
- [ ] No, coverage begins only on endorsement
- [ ] Yes, for the remainder of the policy period
- [ ] Yes, permanently, once premium is adjusted at audit

**Newly acquired organisations** get a short automatic window, which exists so
that an acquisition is not uninsured while the paperwork catches up.

### Which of these does the CGL NOT cover?

- [x] The insured's professional advice to a client
- [ ] A customer's slip in the insured's aisle
- [ ] Injury from a product after it left the insured's possession
- [ ] Wrongful eviction committed by the insured

The CGL covers liability to **third parties** — not employees (workers'
compensation), not vehicles (commercial auto), and **not professional services**,
which need professional liability.

### Which limit caps all damages from one occurrence under Coverages A and C combined?

- [x] The each occurrence limit
- [ ] The general aggregate
- [ ] The damage to premises rented to you limit
- [ ] The medical expense limit

The **each occurrence** limit caps a single event. The **general aggregate** caps
everything in the period except products-completed operations; the **medical
expense** limit is per person under Coverage C; **personal and advertising
injury** is per person or organisation under Coverage B.
