### A claim is excluded by the CGL but covered by the umbrella. The SIR is $25,000 and the loss is $300,000. What does the umbrella pay?

- [x] $275,000
- [ ] $300,000
- [ ] $25,000
- [ ] Nothing, because no underlying policy responds

**The self-insured retention is the amount the insured pays on a claim the
umbrella covers but no underlying policy does.** Here it applies, so the umbrella
pays the balance.

### The insured's CGL aggregate is exhausted in October by earlier claims. A new covered claim arrives in November. What does the umbrella do?

- [x] Drops down to respond in place of the exhausted underlying aggregate
- [ ] Nothing until the policy renews and the aggregate resets
- [ ] Pays only above the original underlying limit
- [ ] Applies the self-insured retention and pays the balance

**Drop-down** is the umbrella's second function, alongside providing excess limits
and covering some losses the underlying policies exclude.

### An umbrella requires $1,000,000 of underlying auto liability. The insured carries $500,000 and has a $3,000,000 loss. What is the insured's share?

- [x] $500,000 — the umbrella responds as though the required underlying limit were in place
- [ ] Nothing; the umbrella drops down to fill the gap
- [ ] $1,000,000, the full required underlying limit
- [ ] $2,500,000, everything above the carried limit

**The umbrella does not become primary just because the primary vanished.** An
insured who lets an underlying policy lapse or carries less than required absorbs
the shortfall.

### Does an excess policy cover a loss the primary excludes?

- [x] No — it follows form, adding limit but not breadth
- [ ] Yes, subject to the self-insured retention
- [ ] Yes, if the exclusion is not repeated in the excess policy
- [ ] Only where the primary aggregate is exhausted

**An excess policy buys height, not breadth.** If the primary excludes something,
so does the excess.

### A personal umbrella insured is sued for slander. The homeowners policy does not cover it. Is the umbrella likely to respond?

- [x] Yes — umbrellas typically cover personal injury offences, subject to the SIR
- [ ] No, an umbrella cannot be broader than the underlying policy
- [ ] Only if the homeowners policy carries a personal injury endorsement
- [ ] Only for written, not oral, defamation

Personal umbrellas cover **libel, slander, false arrest and invasion of privacy**,
which a homeowners policy typically does not.

### A programme has a $1m primary CGL and a $5m umbrella excess of it. A $4m judgment is entered. Who pays what?

- [x] The CGL pays $1m and the umbrella pays $3m
- [ ] The umbrella pays $4m and the CGL nothing
- [ ] The CGL pays $1m and the first excess layer pays $3m
- [ ] Each pays half

**Each layer is only reached when everything beneath it is exhausted.** Excess
layers above the umbrella pay nothing on this judgment.

### What is the single feature that distinguishes an umbrella from an excess policy?

- [x] It can cover losses the underlying policies exclude
- [ ] It carries a higher limit
- [ ] It requires no underlying insurance
- [ ] It is written on an occurrence basis

Both sit above a primary policy. **Only the umbrella broadens coverage**, and that
is the examined distinction.

### When is the self-insured retention irrelevant?

- [x] Where an underlying policy responds and the umbrella simply sits above its limit
- [ ] Where the loss exceeds the umbrella limit
- [ ] Where the claim is made after the policy period
- [ ] Where the insured carries more than the required underlying limits

**The SIR applies only where there is no underlying coverage.** It is not a
deductible in the ordinary sense.

### What underlying limits does a personal umbrella typically require?

- [x] Around $300,000 or $500,000 of liability on each of homeowners and personal auto
- [ ] $1,000,000 on each
- [ ] No underlying coverage at all
- [ ] Underlying limits equal to the umbrella limit

Personal umbrella **limits start at $1,000,000** and are bought in millions. The
cover is cheap relative to its limit because the underlying policies absorb the
frequent small claims and the umbrella sees only the rare severe one.

### Which of these does a personal umbrella typically exclude?

- [x] Business and professional exposures
- [ ] Libel and slander
- [ ] Liability arising from the insured's home
- [ ] Liability arising from the insured's car

Personal umbrellas also exclude **intentional acts** and **the insured's own
injuries**.

### Beyond higher limits, what does a commercial umbrella typically add?

- [x] Personal injury offences not in the underlying form, worldwide coverage, and broader contractual liability
- [ ] Workers' compensation benefits above the statutory level
- [ ] Property coverage for the insured's own buildings
- [ ] Professional liability for the insured's advice

It may also reach certain **watercraft and aircraft** exposures the underlying
policies exclude.

### A programme reads: $1m primary, $5m umbrella, then $10m excess of $6m. Where does the first excess layer attach?

- [x] At $6m, once the primary and umbrella are both exhausted
- [ ] At $1m, alongside the umbrella
- [ ] At $5m, once the umbrella is half exhausted
- [ ] At $11m, above the umbrella's own limit

Layers stack: the primary's $1m plus the umbrella's $5m is $6m, which is exactly
where the next layer begins.
