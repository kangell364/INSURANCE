### An accounting firm owns no vehicles, but its staff drive their own cars to clients. Which symbols does it need?

- [x] 8 and 9 — hired and non-owned autos
- [ ] 1, any auto
- [ ] 2 and 7, owned and specifically described autos
- [ ] 19, mobile equipment subject to financial responsibility law

**Hired and non-owned liability is the coverage a business with no vehicles still
needs**, and the one it most often forgets to buy.

### A repair shop damages a customer's car while test-driving it. Which coverage responds?

- [x] Garagekeepers
- [ ] The business auto physical damage coverage
- [ ] The commercial general liability policy
- [ ] The customer's own collision coverage only

**Damage to customers' autos in the insured's care, custody or control** is
excluded by both the CGL and the business auto policy. Garagekeepers is the
coverage written for it.

### On those facts, which garagekeepers basis pays even if the shop was not negligent and pays before the customer's own insurer?

- [x] Direct primary
- [ ] Direct excess
- [ ] Legal liability
- [ ] Garage liability

**Legal liability requires negligence; the two direct forms do not. Primary pays
first; excess pays only after the customer's own policy is exhausted.**

### Why can physical damage not be written under symbol 1?

- [x] Symbol 1 means any auto, including vehicles the insurer has never seen or rated
- [ ] Symbol 1 applies only to non-owned autos
- [ ] Physical damage requires a compulsory insurance filing
- [ ] Symbol 1 is reserved for motor carriers

**Symbol 1 is available only for liability.** Physical damage uses **2, 3, 4, 7**
or a combination, because an insurer will not insure the hull of a vehicle it
has never seen.

### An executive drives a company car and owns no personal auto policy. She rents a car on holiday and causes an accident. What was needed?

- [x] A Drive Other Car endorsement on the business auto policy
- [ ] Symbol 9, non-owned autos
- [ ] An individual named insured endorsement
- [ ] Nothing; the business auto policy already covers her

**DOC extends liability, medical payments, uninsured motorists and physical
damage to a named individual and their spouse while using autos they do not
own** — other than the company car, which is already covered. It is added
by endorsement for each person.

### A forklift is used only inside the insured's warehouse. Which policy covers liability arising from it?

- [x] The CGL — it is mobile equipment, not an auto
- [ ] The business auto policy, under symbol 2
- [ ] The business auto policy, under symbol 19
- [ ] Workers' compensation

**Symbol 19** addresses the exception: mobile equipment that becomes an auto
because it is **subject to compulsory or financial responsibility law** from
travelling on public roads.

### An employee causes an accident driving their own car on a company errand. Which policy is primary?

- [x] The employee's own policy; the business's non-owned coverage is excess
- [ ] The business auto policy, as the errand was in the scope of employment
- [ ] Both, shared pro rata
- [ ] Neither; personal policies exclude business use

**Non-owned coverage is excess** over the employee's own policy, which remains
primary. The business's liability arises because the employee was acting in the
scope of employment.

### Which symbol covers only autos specifically listed on the declarations?

- [x] 7
- [ ] 2
- [ ] 3
- [ ] 8

**Symbol 7 covers only what is listed**, so a newly purchased vehicle must be
reported. **Symbols 2, 3 and 4 pick up newly acquired autos automatically.**

### Are vehicles borrowed from employees "hired autos"?

- [x] No — hired autos are leased, rented, hired or borrowed, but not from employees
- [ ] Yes, any borrowed vehicle is a hired auto
- [ ] Yes, if the business pays the employee for use of the vehicle
- [ ] Only where the borrowing exceeds 30 days

An employee's own car used for the business is a **non-owned** auto. Hired auto
premium is based on **cost of hire**, and hired coverage is usually excess over
the owner's own insurance.

### An owner-operator uses the truck for personal purposes on a weekend, outside the motor carrier's business. Which coverage responds?

- [x] Non-trucking liability, sometimes called bobtail
- [ ] Motor truck cargo
- [ ] Trailer interchange
- [ ] The federal filing endorsement

**Non-trucking liability** covers the owner-operator's liability while the truck
is used **outside** the motor carrier's business — exactly the gap the carrier's
own policy leaves.

### Which coverage addresses physical damage to another party's trailers held under an interchange agreement?

- [x] Trailer interchange
- [ ] Motor truck cargo
- [ ] Garagekeepers direct excess
- [ ] Hired auto physical damage

**Motor truck cargo** covers the goods being hauled, written as inland marine.
**Trailer interchange** covers the trailers themselves.

### A sole proprietor uses the same pickup for the business and for family errands. Which endorsement brings the business auto policy close to personal auto coverage?

- [x] Individual named insured
- [ ] Drive Other Car
- [ ] Symbol 1 on all coverages
- [ ] Hired and non-owned liability

The **individual named insured** endorsement broadens a business auto policy for
an individual whose business and personal use are the same vehicles, reaching
family members and non-owned vehicles.

### A business wants its vehicles' downtime covered after a physical damage loss. What is available?

- [x] Loss of use and rental reimbursement, by endorsement
- [ ] Nothing; commercial auto covers repair cost only
- [ ] Automatic coverage under the collision limit
- [ ] Coverage under the CGL's supplementary payments

Business auto physical damage also includes **towing and labour at the place of
disablement**, and **transportation expenses following theft**. Loss of use and
rental reimbursement are **endorsements**, not defaults.
