### A contractor is bonded on a school project. Who is the principal, who the obligee, who the surety?

- [x] The contractor, the school district, and the bonding company
- [ ] The school district, the contractor, and the bonding company
- [ ] The bonding company, the contractor, and the school district
- [ ] The contractor, the bonding company, and the school district

**The principal** is whose performance is guaranteed; **the obligee** is the party
protected, who required the bond; **the surety** guarantees the principal to the
obligee.

### A contractor abandons a job half-finished. The surety pays the owner $400,000 to complete it. Does the contractor owe anything?

- [x] Yes — the full $400,000; the principal must indemnify the surety
- [ ] No, the premium paid for the guarantee
- [ ] Only the amount above the bond penalty
- [ ] Only if the abandonment was deliberate

**An insured never repays an insurer; a principal always repays a surety.** That
is why suretyship is underwritten as a credit decision and any payment is treated
as an advance.

### A contractor submits the low bid and then refuses to sign. Which bond, and what does it protect against?

- [x] The bid bond, protecting the owner against the extra cost of going to the next bidder
- [ ] The performance bond, protecting against non-completion
- [ ] The payment bond, protecting subcontractors
- [ ] The maintenance bond, protecting against defects

**The bid bond gets you the contract, the performance bond finishes the work, the
payment bond pays the subcontractors.**

### Subcontractors are unpaid and threaten liens on the owner's property. Which bond?

- [x] The payment bond, also called labour and material
- [ ] The performance bond
- [ ] The completion bond
- [ ] The supply bond

A **completion bond** is given to a **lender** rather than an owner, guaranteeing
the project is finished free of liens. A **supply bond** guarantees delivery of
materials under a supply contract.

### Four employees conspire to steal $200,000. The employer has a $75,000 blanket position bond. Roughly what is available?

- [x] $300,000 — the limit applies to each employee
- [ ] $75,000 — one limit for the scheme
- [ ] $150,000 — two limits
- [ ] $200,000 — the amount of the loss, capped at the total limit

**Blanket position applies the limit to each employee; commercial blanket applies
it per loss** however many were involved. Blanket position provides more coverage
for the same limit where employees collude.

### Why does a surety expect no losses?

- [x] The bond is a credit judgement, and any payment is recoverable from the principal
- [ ] Bond penalties are set below the expected loss
- [ ] Surety bonds exclude the most common causes of default
- [ ] Obligees must exhaust other remedies first

**Bond underwriting examines the principal's character, capacity and capital** —
credit, experience, financial statements, work in progress. It is risk avoidance,
not risk assumption.

### Which fidelity form covers whoever happens to occupy a role, without naming the person?

- [x] Position schedule
- [ ] Name schedule
- [ ] Commercial blanket
- [ ] Blanket position

**Name schedule** covers named individuals with stated amounts, and adding a
person requires notifying the surety. **Position schedule** covers the position.
**Blanket** forms cover all employees without naming anybody.

### An executor is appointed to administer an estate. What kind of bond is required?

- [x] A fiduciary bond
- [ ] A judicial bond
- [ ] A public official bond
- [ ] A licence and permit bond

**Fiduciary bonds** guarantee that a person appointed by a court to manage
another's affairs — executor, administrator, guardian, trustee or receiver — does
so faithfully. **Judicial bonds** guarantee a litigating party's obligations:
appeal, attachment, injunction, replevin.

### An insurance producer in many states must carry which kind of bond?

- [x] A licence and permit bond
- [ ] A fidelity bond
- [ ] A public official bond
- [ ] A bid bond

**Licence and permit bonds** are required by a public authority before a licence
issues, guaranteeing compliance with the law governing the activity and
compensating those harmed by non-compliance.

### How does bond premium differ conceptually from insurance premium?

- [x] It is a service fee for the guarantee rather than a rate for assumed risk
- [ ] It is calculated as a percentage of the obligee's expected loss
- [ ] It is refundable if the principal performs
- [ ] It is paid by the obligee rather than the principal

Insurance prices **expected losses** into the rate. A surety prices a **guarantee**
it does not expect to fund, and bonds are often non-cancellable for their term
where a policy usually is not.

### What does a maintenance bond guarantee?

- [x] The work against defects for a stated period after completion
- [ ] That subcontractors will be paid after the job closes
- [ ] That the contractor will maintain required insurance during the job
- [ ] That the owner will maintain the finished structure

It sits after the performance bond's job is done, covering the period when defects
in completed work come to light.

### What is the difference between a fidelity bond and employee dishonesty insurance?

- [x] A fidelity bond guarantees the honesty of a named person or position; employee dishonesty insurance covers the employer's loss from employees generally
- [ ] A fidelity bond covers only theft of money; employee dishonesty covers all property
- [ ] A fidelity bond is issued by the state; employee dishonesty is issued by an insurer
- [ ] There is no difference; the terms are interchangeable

Fidelity is the class of bond closest to insurance, which is exactly why the exam
tests the distinction.
