### An insurer fails. A homeowner has a $450,000 covered fire claim, within the policy limit. What does the association pay?

- [x] $300,000
- [ ] $450,000
- [ ] $500,000
- [ ] Nothing — the association pays only workers' compensation

**§462.213(a)** caps an individual covered claim at **$300,000**, even where the
policy would have paid more. The association steps into the insurer's shoes, and
then only up to its own cap.

### The same insurer wrote a workers' compensation policy, and an injured worker's benefits will exceed $300,000. What is paid?

- [x] The full amount — workers' compensation covered claims are not capped
- [ ] $300,000, the same as any other claim
- [ ] $300,000, plus medical benefits above that
- [ ] Nothing; workers' compensation is outside the chapter

**§462.213(b)** requires the association to pay **the full amount** of a covered
claim arising out of a workers' compensation policy. **Everything else is capped
at $300,000; workers' compensation is paid in full.**

### Three members of one family are injured in a single accident and each claims $200,000 against an impaired insurer's liability policy. What is available in total?

- [x] $300,000 — derivative claims from one occurrence are a single claim
- [ ] $900,000 — three separate claims
- [ ] $600,000 — two claims plus a partial third
- [ ] $300,000 each, so $900,000

**§462.213(c)** treats derivative claims by more than one person arising from the
same occurrence **collectively as a single claim**, which closes the obvious route
around the cap.

### A company with a consolidated net worth of $80 million has a covered property claim against an impaired insurer. What does the association pay?

- [x] Nothing — the net worth exclusion applies
- [ ] $300,000
- [ ] The full claim, since net worth is irrelevant to property claims
- [ ] Half the claim

**§462.212** excludes claims arising from the policy of an insured whose net worth
exceeded **$50 million** on 31 December of the year before the insurer became
impaired. The association protects those who cannot absorb an insurer's failure.

### Same company, but the claim is under its workers' compensation policy. Does the answer change?

- [x] Yes — workers' compensation is expressly carved out of the net worth exclusion
- [ ] No, the exclusion applies to every line
- [ ] Yes, but the claim is capped at $300,000
- [ ] No, unless the company waives its other remedies

Workers' compensation is carved out **twice**: out of the cap in §462.213(b) and
out of the net worth exclusion in §462.212. An injured worker's benefits do not
depend on the size of their employer.

### How is net worth measured for that exclusion?

- [x] On a consolidated basis, including the insured's parent, subsidiary and affiliated companies
- [ ] For the named insured entity alone
- [ ] By the insured's Texas operations only
- [ ] By the policy limit purchased

**§462.212(b).** If the insured refuses to provide the financial information
requested, the association **may deem** net worth to exceed $50 million — and in
any lawsuit contesting it the **insured bears the burden of proof** and pays the
association's costs and fees.

### A policyholder paid a year's premium and the insurer fails after two months. Can they recover the unused portion?

- [x] Yes, as a covered claim for unearned premium, up to $25,000
- [ ] No, only claims for loss are covered
- [ ] Yes, without limit
- [ ] Yes, up to $300,000 like any other claim

**§462.202(a)** makes unearned premium a covered claim with its own, much smaller
cap.

### A business insured through a surplus lines insurer that becomes insolvent asks the association to pay. Will it?

- [x] No — a surplus lines insurer is not a member of the association
- [ ] Yes, on the same terms as an admitted insurer
- [ ] Yes, up to $300,000
- [ ] Yes, but only for workers' compensation claims

That is the real price of the surplus lines market, and **§981.101(b)** requires
the surplus lines document to say so **in 11-point type**.

### Which of these is NOT required for a claim to be a covered claim?

- [x] That the claimant first exhaust any other insurance available to them
- [ ] That the claim be unpaid
- [ ] That the issuing insurer be an impaired insurer
- [ ] That the claim be within the coverage and applicable limits of the policy

**§462.201** lists five requirements: unpaid; under a policy issued by an
authorized insurer; arising out of and within the policy's coverage and limits;
the insurer is impaired; and the claimant is a **Texas resident at the time of the
insured event** or the claim is first-party for **property permanently located in
Texas**.

### A corporation claims against the association. Where is it a resident?

- [x] The state in which its principal place of business is located
- [ ] The state of its incorporation
- [ ] Every state in which it does business
- [ ] The state where the policy was issued

**§462.205** settles it for any non-individual entity.

### May an affiliate of the impaired insurer bring a claim?

- [x] No — §462.204 bars it
- [ ] Yes, on the same terms as any other claimant
- [ ] Yes, but capped at $25,000
- [ ] Only for workers' compensation claims

**§462.308** goes further: the association may **recover** covered claims and
defence costs it paid on behalf of an affiliate of the impaired insurer.

### How much may a member insurer be assessed in a year?

- [x] No more than two percent of its net direct written premiums for the preceding calendar year
- [ ] No more than five percent of its net direct written premiums
- [ ] An unlimited amount, in proportion to market share
- [ ] $1 million

**§462.160 and §462.168.** Membership is compulsory — it is the industry insuring
itself against its own failures, the same structure as TWIA and the FAIR Plan.

### A claimant could recover a workers' compensation claim from more than one state's guaranty association. Which does it go to first?

- [x] The association of the claimant's residence
- [ ] The association of the insurer's domicile
- [ ] The association of the state where the injury occurred
- [ ] Whichever the claimant chooses

**§462.254(c).** A **first-party property** claim goes first to the association
where the **property is located**; other claims go first to the association of the
**insured's residence**.
