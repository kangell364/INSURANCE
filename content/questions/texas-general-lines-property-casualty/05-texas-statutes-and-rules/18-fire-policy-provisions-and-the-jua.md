### A building insured for $300,000 is a total loss by fire. Its actual cash value was $220,000. What is owed?

- [x] $300,000 — the full policy amount
- [ ] $220,000, the actual cash value
- [ ] $260,000, the average of the two
- [ ] The cost to rebuild, whatever that proves to be

**§862.053(a)** makes a total fire loss of real property a **liquidated demand
against the company for the full amount of such policy.** The limit becomes the
measure of the loss — the opposite of the ordinary indemnity position.

### Same policy, but the fire causes $60,000 of damage and the building stands. What is owed?

- [x] The loss settled the ordinary way
- [ ] $300,000, the full policy amount
- [ ] $60,000 plus a proportionate share of the limit
- [ ] Nothing, because the rule applies only to total losses

The rule requires a **total** loss. A partial fire loss is settled normally, on
whatever basis the policy provides.

### Same building, a total loss, but caused by a tornado. Does §862.053 apply?

- [x] No — the rule requires a loss by fire
- [ ] Yes, any total loss of real property triggers it
- [ ] Yes, if windstorm is a covered peril
- [ ] Only if the policy is written by TWIA

Three conditions must all hold: **a total loss, by fire, of real property.**

### A total fire loss destroys $40,000 of business contents insured for $75,000. What does the valued policy rule give?

- [x] Nothing extra — the rule expressly does not apply to personal property
- [ ] $75,000, the full contents limit
- [ ] $57,500, a proportionate amount
- [ ] $40,000 plus the difference to the limit

**§862.053(a)** says so in terms: *"This subsection does not apply to personal
property."*

### Where must the liquidated demand provision appear?

- [x] Verbatim in each fire insurance policy issued as coverage on real property in Texas
- [ ] Only in the declarations
- [ ] Only in policies above a stated limit
- [ ] Nowhere; it operates by statute without appearing in the policy

**§862.053(b)**, and **§862.053(c)** makes the commissioner require compliance.
The insured must be able to read the rule in their own policy.

### An insured breached a condition of a fire policy on personal property, and the breach had nothing to do with the fire. What is the effect?

- [x] The breach does not have the effect it otherwise would, because it did not contribute to cause the destruction
- [ ] The policy is void from inception
- [ ] The claim is reduced proportionally to the seriousness of the breach
- [ ] Coverage continues but the insurer may cancel immediately

**§862.054** is a causation rule — Texas softening the strict warranty doctrine
Module 2 described, where breach voided a policy with no causal connection at
all.

### A client insures a $200,000 building for $400,000 and it burns to the ground. Why is that a problem?

- [x] The statute makes the demand liquidated at the policy amount, so over-insuring is not harmless in Texas
- [ ] The insurer will pay only the actual cash value regardless
- [ ] The policy is void for over-insurance
- [ ] Coinsurance reduces the payment to half

It is also why an insurer underwrites the **amount of insurance** on a fire
policy rather than accepting whatever the applicant asks for.

### Which insurers must belong to the Medical Liability JUA?

- [x] Every insurer authorized to write and writing liability insurance, including auto liability, on a direct basis in Texas — except farm and county mutuals
- [ ] Only insurers writing medical liability insurance
- [ ] Only physicians' mutual insurance companies
- [ ] Membership is voluntary

**§2203.055(a)** — and note how wide that is: **writing any liability insurance,
including auto, makes an insurer a member**, whether or not it writes medical
liability at all. **§2203.055(b)** makes membership a condition of authority.

### Which two kinds of insurer are excepted from JUA membership?

- [x] Farm mutual companies under Chapter 911 and county mutual companies under Chapter 912
- [ ] Lloyd's plans and reciprocal exchanges
- [ ] Surplus lines insurers and risk retention groups
- [ ] Domestic stock companies and fraternal benefit societies

**Lloyd's plans and reciprocals are expressly included**, not excepted — which
is the distractor this question sets.

### What is the JUA's purpose?

- [x] To provide medical liability insurance on a self-supporting basis
- [ ] To pay claims against insolvent medical liability insurers
- [ ] To set medical liability rates for the voluntary market
- [ ] To license physicians and health care providers

**§2203.051.** Paying claims against insolvent insurers is the **guaranty
association's** job, under Chapter 462 — a different body with a different
purpose.

### Does the JUA's existence prevent a physician buying medical liability cover from a surplus lines insurer?

- [x] No — §2203.005 says the association is not an authorized insurer for Chapter 981 purposes
- [ ] Yes, the JUA must decline before surplus lines may be used
- [ ] Yes, surplus lines may not write medical liability at all
- [ ] Only where the physician has been declined twice

Chapter 981's diligent effort needs a declination from an insurer **authorized
and actually writing** the class. Because the JUA is not an authorized insurer
for this purpose, **it neither satisfies nor obstructs** that requirement.

### Who decides which physicians and health care providers may obtain JUA coverage?

- [x] The commissioner, by order, and the order may be revised to include or exclude categories
- [ ] The board of directors, by resolution
- [ ] The Texas Medical Association
- [ ] The statute fixes the categories

**§2203.101(a).** Eligibility is set by order rather than written into the
statute, so it can move. **§2203.102** separately makes the association the
insurer of last resort for certain **nursing homes and assisted living
facilities**.
