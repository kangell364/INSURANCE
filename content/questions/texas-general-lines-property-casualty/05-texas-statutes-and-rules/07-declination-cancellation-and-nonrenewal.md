### A commercial property policy is in its first term, issued 100 days ago. The underwriter wants out because the risk looks worse than expected. May the insurer cancel?

- [x] No — §551.052(b) bars cancellation after the 60th day of the initial term, and second thoughts is not a listed ground
- [ ] Yes, with 10 days' written notice
- [ ] Yes, with 60 days' written notice
- [ ] Yes, but only at the next anniversary

**There is a 60-day underwriting window and then the door closes.** After it, the
grounds are limited to fraud in obtaining coverage, failure to pay premium, an
increase in hazard within the insured's control, and loss of reinsurance — plus
supervision, conservatorship or receivership under §551.052(d).

### Same policy, but the insured has not paid the premium. May the insurer cancel, and with how much notice?

- [x] Yes — 10 days' written notice to the first-named insured
- [ ] Yes — 30 days' written notice
- [ ] Yes — 60 days' written notice
- [ ] No, non-payment allows non-renewal only

**Texas sets its own numbers: 10 days for cancellation, 60 days for
non-renewal** — not the generic 10/30/30 of the general pattern. Notice goes to
the first-named insured at the address shown on the policy.

### A homeowners insurer mails non-renewal notice 30 days before expiry. What happens?

- [x] Coverage remains in effect until the 61st day after the notice was delivered or mailed
- [ ] The non-renewal is void and the policy must be renewed
- [ ] The non-renewal takes effect at expiry anyway
- [ ] The insurer must pay a penalty but the non-renewal stands

**Late notice does not void the non-renewal; it postpones it.** Earned premium
for the extended period is computed **pro rata based on the previous year's
rate**.

### A personal auto policy has been in force 8 months. The named insured's son, who lives at home, has his licence revoked. What are the insurer's options?

- [x] Cancel on 10 days' notice, unless the named insured consents to an endorsement excluding the son
- [ ] Cancel immediately with no notice
- [ ] Non-renew at expiry, but not cancel mid-term
- [ ] Nothing; a household member's licence is not a ground

**§551.104(d)** reaches the named insured, any operator resident in the same
household, and anyone who customarily drives a covered auto — but **the
exclusion endorsement defeats it**.

### A homeowners policy is 40 days old. The insurer received an inspection report 20 days ago and said nothing. May it now cancel relying on that report?

- [x] No — under §551.104(h) the report is considered accepted after the 11th day
- [ ] Yes, the policy is still within the 60-day window
- [ ] Yes, provided it gives 10 days' notice
- [ ] Yes, if the report was dated within 90 days of the effective date

**Silence is acceptance.** The insurer had to reject the report before the 11th
day after receiving it.

### An insurer cancels a homeowners policy on a ground not listed in §551.104. What is the effect?

- [x] The cancellation is ineffective and the policy remains in force
- [ ] The cancellation stands, but the insurer owes an administrative penalty
- [ ] The cancellation is postponed to the 61st day
- [ ] The insured may sue for treble damages but the policy ends

**§551.111.** The grounds in §551.104 are a closed list — "an insurer may cancel
only as provided by this section".

### An insurer quietly narrows a personal auto policy by endorsement mid-term, without the insured's consent. What has it done?

- [x] Cancelled the policy, under §551.103
- [ ] Nothing requiring notice; the policy remains in force as amended
- [ ] Non-renewed the policy early
- [ ] Declined additional coverage, which is unregulated

**Point 3 of §551.103 is the one that catches people.** Terminating coverage,
refusing additional coverage the insured is entitled to, and **reducing or
restricting coverage by endorsement or other means** are all cancellation.

### Under §551.105, what happens if an insurer sends no non-renewal notice?

- [x] The insurer must renew the policy at the insured's request — silence means renewal
- [ ] The policy expires as scheduled
- [ ] The policy continues month to month until notice is given
- [ ] The insured may demand coverage from TWIA

**The default runs the other way from most states.** The notice must go out not
later than the **60th day** before expiry, and it covers renewal with a change in
coverage under §2002.001 as well as outright non-renewal.

### How long must a policy other than personal auto or homeowners have been in effect before the new-business cancellation window closes?

- [x] 90 days
- [ ] 60 days
- [ ] 30 days
- [ ] 120 days

**§551.104(g)** sets three periods: **90 days** for other policies, **60 days**
for personal automobile, and **60 days** for homeowners — the last with two
further conditions attached.

### Within the homeowners 60-day window, what further condition must the insurer meet?

- [x] Identify an undisclosed condition creating increased hazard that is not the subject of a prior claim, or decline an inspection report dated within 90 days of the effective date
- [ ] Obtain the Commissioner's approval for the cancellation
- [ ] Give 60 rather than 10 days' notice
- [ ] Offer the insured replacement coverage with an affiliate

The inspection report must have been completed by an inspector licensed by the
Texas Real Estate Commission or otherwise authorised.

### When may an insurer cancel a personal automobile policy outside the new-business window and the listed grounds?

- [x] Effective on any 12-month anniversary, on notice mailed not later than the 60th day before
- [ ] Never; the policy must be renewed indefinitely
- [ ] At any time, on 10 days' notice
- [ ] Only at the insured's request

**§551.104(f)** is the escape hatch, and the 60-day notice is what makes it fair.

### What must an insurer's statement of reasons for a declination, cancellation or non-renewal contain?

- [x] A full explanation, the precise incident or risk factors violating the insurer's guidelines, and the source of the information relied on
- [ ] A citation to the statutory ground only
- [ ] The name of the underwriter who made the decision
- [ ] Nothing; reasons need only be given on the insured's written request

**§551.002.** And **§551.003 and §551.110** make the insurer, agent and employee
**immune from liability for statements made in good faith** in giving those
reasons — without which no insurer would give a candid one.

### Is transferring a policy to another insurer within the same group a refusal to renew?

- [x] No — §551.004 says it is not
- [ ] Yes, and it requires 60 days' notice
- [ ] Yes, unless the insured consents in writing
- [ ] Only where the new insurer's rates are higher

A group reshuffle is not the insured being turned away.

### May an insurer cancel or refuse to renew a policy because the insured holds elected office?

- [x] No — §551.152 prohibits it
- [ ] Yes, elected officials present a heightened liability exposure
- [ ] Only for liability lines
- [ ] Only with the Commissioner's approval

**§551.113** separately prohibits declination, non-renewal or cancellation on
certain grounds relating to the **consideration of consumer information** — the
Texas counterpart to the FCRA material in Module 2.
