### May the Commissioner issue an emergency cease and desist order without a hearing?

- [x] Yes — §83.051(a) authorises it ex parte
- [ ] No, a hearing always precedes a cease and desist order
- [ ] Only with the written agreement of the affected person
- [ ] Only where the State Office of Administrative Hearings approves in advance

*Ex parte* means **without the other side present: no hearing beforehand**. It is
the exception to the hearing-first rule, which is exactly why it is heavily
tested.

### A producer is served with an emergency order and requests a hearing on day 20. May they carry on the conduct until the hearing?

- [x] No — under §83.055 the order stays in effect pending the hearing
- [ ] Yes, requesting a hearing suspends the order
- [ ] Yes, for 30 days after the request
- [ ] Only if the conduct is not alleged to be fraudulent

**Act first, hear later, and the order bites throughout.** The order is served by
registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, and requires the person to
cease **immediately**.

### What is the general cap on an administrative penalty under §84.022(a)?

- [x] $25,000 per violation, unless a greater or lesser penalty is specified elsewhere in the insurance laws
- [ ] $25,000 for all violations arising from one course of conduct
- [ ] $5,000 per violation, with no exceptions
- [ ] $1,000 per violation, doubled for intentional conduct

**$25,000 per violation is the headline figure**, and the trailing clause is what
makes §541.110's $1,000 cap possible: a specific provision displaces the general
one.

### A person disobeys a cease and desist order issued under §541.108. What is the maximum administrative penalty for all violations?

- [x] $5,000
- [ ] $1,000
- [ ] $25,000
- [ ] $50,000

§541.110(c) caps it at **$1,000 per violation and $5,000 for all violations**.
The numbers are small on purpose: they punish **disobeying the order**, not the
underlying conduct, which is punished under Chapter 84.

### The Commissioner orders a producer to repay $40,000 taken from clients and separately imposes a penalty. What is the $40,000 called, and who receives it?

- [x] Restitution under §82.053, paid to the harmed parties
- [ ] An administrative penalty, paid to the state
- [ ] A civil penalty, paid to the state
- [ ] A forfeiture, paid to the Department's operating fund

**Restitution is not a penalty**: it returns money to the person who lost it, and
it can be ordered alongside a penalty that goes to the state.

### A Texas licence is revoked. Does that stay in Texas?

- [x] No — under §82.056 the Commissioner shall give notice to other states
- [ ] Yes, revocations have no effect outside Texas
- [ ] Only if the producer is licensed in a compact state
- [ ] Only where the revocation was for fraud

**A Texas revocation follows a producer across state lines**, which is why an
administrative action in any state must be reported everywhere the producer is
licensed.

### Under §84.041(b), how soon must the Department give written notice of a penalty report?

- [x] Not later than the 14th day after the report is issued
- [ ] Not later than the 20th day after the report is issued
- [ ] Not later than the 30th day after the report is issued
- [ ] Within 60 days, by certified mail

The three clocks under Chapter 84 are **14, 20 and 30**: 14 days to give notice
of the report, **20 days** for the person to accept or request a hearing under
§84.042(a), and **30 days** after a final order to pay or petition for judicial
review under §84.045.

### On what day does an emergency cease and desist order become final if no hearing is requested?

- [x] The 61st day after service
- [ ] The 30th day after service
- [ ] The 20th day after service
- [ ] Immediately on service

The person must request a hearing **not later than the 60th day** after being
served, under §83.053(b); §83.051(b) makes the order final on the **61st day**
if they do not.

### Which is NOT among the grounds for an emergency cease and desist order under Chapter 83?

- [x] The person has failed to pay an administrative penalty when due
- [ ] The conduct is fraudulent
- [ ] The conduct is hazardous or creates an immediate danger to public safety
- [ ] The conduct is causing or may reasonably be expected to cause public injury of the kind described in the statute

The order is available where the Commissioner believes an authorized person is
committing an unfair act or is in a hazardous financial condition **and** the
conduct meets one of those three descriptions — and also against an
**unauthorized person** engaging in the business of insurance in violation of
Chapter 101.

### Which factor does §84.022(b) list in setting the amount of a penalty?

- [x] Whether the violation was intentional
- [ ] Whether the person holds licences in other states
- [ ] The person's gross annual revenue
- [ ] The number of policies the person has placed

The full list: **seriousness, the hazard created, economic harm to the public
interest, history of previous violations, the amount necessary to deter, efforts
to correct, and whether the violation was intentional.**

### What does "authorization" mean in Chapter 82?

- [x] A permit, licence or certificate of authority — so the chapter reaches both companies and producers
- [ ] A producer's licence only
- [ ] An insurer's certificate of authority only
- [ ] The Commissioner's written approval of a policy form

**§82.051** allows cancellation or revocation **after notice and opportunity for
a hearing**; **§82.052** allows other sanctions instead of or in addition to
revocation.

### Does a prosecution under the Insurance Code displace the Penal Code?

- [x] No — §85.052 permits prosecution under this code or any other law of the state
- [ ] Yes, the Insurance Code is the exclusive criminal remedy for insurance offences
- [ ] Yes, unless the Commissioner refers the matter to a district attorney
- [ ] Only where the amount involved exceeds $25,000

**Theft and fraud remain available.** §85.001 makes a violation for which no
other punishment is provided an offence in itself.

### After a hearing and a determination of violation under §541.107, what does §541.108 require the Department to do?

- [x] Make written findings and issue a cease and desist order — the word is "shall"
- [ ] Consider whether an order is warranted, in its discretion
- [ ] Refer the matter to SOAH for a second hearing
- [ ] Impose an administrative penalty before any order issues

**On finding a violation, the order is not discretionary.** §541.109 lets the
Department modify or set the order aside at any time before an appeal is filed.
