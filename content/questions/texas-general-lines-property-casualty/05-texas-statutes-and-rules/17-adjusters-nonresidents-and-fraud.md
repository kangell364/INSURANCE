### A person investigates losses for an insurer as an independent contractor. Do they need an adjuster's licence?

- [x] Yes — the definition expressly includes an independent contractor acting on behalf of an insurer
- [ ] No, only employees of insurers need a licence
- [ ] No, independent contractors are exempt
- [ ] Only if they adjust more than ten claims a year

**§4101.001(a)(1)** reaches a person investigating or adjusting losses **on behalf
of an insurer** as an independent contractor or as an employee of an adjustment
bureau, association, agent, independent contractor, insurer or managing general
agent. **§4101.051** requires the licence.

### What anchors the statutory definition of an adjuster?

- [x] Acting on behalf of an insurer
- [ ] Being paid a fee rather than a salary
- [ ] Holding a property and casualty agent licence
- [ ] Investigating losses above a stated dollar amount

A **public adjuster**, who acts for the **insured**, is a different licence under a
different chapter. A fact pattern turning on whose side the adjuster is on is
testing exactly this.

### An applicant for an adjuster's licence lives in another state. Can they be licensed in Texas?

- [x] Yes, if their state or country permits a Texas resident to act as an adjuster there
- [ ] No, adjusters must reside in Texas
- [ ] Yes, without condition
- [ ] Only if they also hold a Texas agent licence

**§4101.053(a)(2)(B)** — a reciprocity test. The same section requires the
applicant to be **at least 18 years of age**.

### An agent licensed and in good standing in New Mexico applies for a Texas non-resident licence. What decides the outcome?

- [x] Whether New Mexico grants non-resident licences to Texas agents on a reciprocal basis
- [ ] Whether the applicant passes the Texas examination
- [ ] Whether the applicant has a Texas appointment in place
- [ ] Whether the applicant maintains an office in Texas

**§4056.052(a)** — **reciprocity is the whole mechanism.** Texas gives what it
gets.

### What rights does a non-resident licence holder have?

- [x] The same rights and privileges as a resident holder, except as the code specifically provides
- [ ] Only the right to service existing Texas business
- [ ] Only the right to write commercial lines
- [ ] Rights determined case by case by the Department

**§4056.002.** And **§4056.001** applies the rest of the title's licensing
provisions to them, so appointment, monthly notification and separate records are
not escaped by being out of state.

### An agent's Texas licence expired eight months ago and was never suspended or revoked. Must they re-sit the examination?

- [x] No — the licence expired less than one year ago and was not denied, revoked or suspended
- [ ] Yes, any expired licence requires re-examination
- [ ] Yes, unless the agent completed continuing education
- [ ] No, and no new licence is needed either

**§4002.003(a)(2)** is what actually delivers §4003.007's middle band: more than
90 days but less than a year after expiry produces a **new licence without
re-examination**.

### Same facts, except the previous licence had been revoked. Does the answer change?

- [x] Yes — the exemption requires that the previous licence was not denied, revoked or suspended
- [ ] No, the one-year rule applies regardless
- [ ] No, but an additional fee is payable
- [ ] Yes, and no licence may be issued at all

That qualifier is easy to miss and is exactly what the question exists to test.

### A producer forms a reasonable suspicion on 1 June that a claim is fraudulent. What must they do, and by when?

- [x] Report it in writing to the Department's insurance fraud unit not later than the 30th day — by 1 July
- [ ] Report it to the local prosecuting attorney within 10 days
- [ ] Report it to the insurer's own antifraud unit within 60 days
- [ ] Nothing until the suspicion is confirmed

**§701.051(a).** Three things to hold: the trigger is **reasonable suspicion**, the
clock is **30 days**, and the report goes to the **insurance fraud unit** in
**writing**, in the format the unit or the **NAIC** prescribes.

### Must the producer be sure the fraud occurred before reporting?

- [x] No — the duty is triggered by a determination or reasonable suspicion, and reaches an act about to be committed
- [ ] Yes, a report requires documented proof
- [ ] Yes, unless the insurer authorises the report
- [ ] No, but only for acts already completed

**§701.051(a)** covers an act that **has been or is about to be committed** in this
state.

### What is the purpose of the Department's insurance fraud unit?

- [x] To enforce laws relating to fraudulent insurance acts
- [ ] To adjust disputed claims between insurers and insureds
- [ ] To license adjusters and investigators
- [ ] To assess the guaranty association

**§701.101(a).** It may also **receive, review and investigate** insurer antifraud
reports submitted under **Chapter 704**, and reports **annually in writing** to the
commissioner.

### Who is an "authorized governmental agency" under Chapter 701?

- [x] Municipal, county, state or federal law enforcement agencies, and prosecuting attorneys of a municipality, county or judicial district
- [ ] The Texas Department of Insurance only
- [ ] The National Association of Insurance Commissioners
- [ ] Any licensed private investigator engaged by an insurer

**§701.001(1)**, and it reaches agencies and prosecutors **of this state or another
state or of the United States**.

### Which applicants must take the written examination under §4002.001?

- [x] Applicants for an agent's licence, except as the code otherwise provides
- [ ] Only applicants for a general lines property and casualty licence
- [ ] Only applicants who have not held a licence in another state
- [ ] Only applicants for a permanent, not a temporary, licence

**§4002.002** separately provides a **limited written examination** for a limited
agent's licence under Chapter 4051 or 4054, and **§4001.152** requires **no written
examination for a temporary licence** — which is the point of a temporary licence.
