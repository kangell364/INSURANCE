### An adjuster tells a claimant the policy excludes their loss when it does not. Which section?

- [x] §541.060(a)(1) — misrepresenting a material fact or policy provision relating to coverage
- [ ] §541.053, defamation of an insurer
- [ ] §541.054, boycott, coercion or intimidation
- [ ] §541.055, false financial statement

§541.061 also reaches it, as an **untrue statement of material fact**
misrepresenting a policy.

### An insurer's liability is clear but it delays settlement to pressure the claimant on an unrelated part of the claim. Which section?

- [x] §541.060(a)(2) — failing to attempt in good faith a prompt, fair and equitable settlement
- [ ] §541.051, misrepresentation regarding a policy
- [ ] §541.059, deceptive name or slogan
- [ ] §541.052, false information and advertising

The subsection expressly reaches **using one clear coverage to pressure
settlement of another**. **"Reasonably clear" is the operative phrase**: the duty
attaches once liability is reasonably clear, not once it is proved.

### A pedestrian injured by an insured sues the insured's liability insurer under §541.060. Will it succeed?

- [x] No — §541.060(b) denies a cause of action to a third party claiming against an insured under a liability policy
- [ ] Yes, Chapter 541 reaches any person who sustains actual damages
- [ ] Yes, but without treble damages
- [ ] Only if the insured assigns their rights to the claimant

This is the chapter's most important limitation. The claimant sues the insured;
they do not get to sue the insured's insurer under §541.060.

### A jury finds an insurer knowingly committed an unfair practice causing $200,000 of actual damages. What is the maximum award of damages?

- [x] $600,000
- [ ] $200,000
- [ ] $400,000
- [ ] $200,000 plus costs only

**§541.152(b)** permits **up to three times actual damages** on a finding that
the defendant acted **knowingly**, plus court costs and reasonable and necessary
attorney's fees. §541.002(1) defines "knowingly" as **actual awareness**, which
may be inferred from objective manifestations.

### Same finding, but the defendant is the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association. What changes?

- [x] No treble damages — §541.152(c) excludes actions against TWIA
- [ ] Nothing; TWIA is treated as any other insurer
- [ ] Damages are capped at the policy limit
- [ ] The action must be brought in Travis County

TWIA cannot be hit with treble damages, which is a deliberate legislative choice
about a residual market funded by the industry.

### An insured discovers in March 2026 an unfair practice that occurred in 2021. Is the action time-barred?

- [x] Not necessarily — the two-year period runs from occurrence or from discovery
- [ ] Yes, the period runs strictly from the date of the practice
- [ ] Yes, unless the insurer concealed the practice
- [ ] No, there is no limitations period under Chapter 541

**§541.162** runs two years from the practice **or** from the date the plaintiff
discovered it or should have with reasonable diligence — **extendable by 180
days** where the defendant's conduct was solely calculated to induce delay.

### An insurer demands a claimant's tax returns to settle a stolen-vehicle claim. Permitted?

- [x] No — §541.060(a)(9) permits it only on a court order, for a fire loss, or where lost profits or income are claimed
- [ ] Yes, an insurer may verify a claimant's finances in any claim
- [ ] Yes, for any theft claim above $10,000
- [ ] Yes, provided the claimant consents in writing

Three exceptions, and a stolen vehicle is none of them.

### What must a plaintiff do before filing a Chapter 541 action?

- [x] Give written notice not later than the 61st day before filing, stating the complaint and the amount of damages and expenses
- [ ] Exhaust the Department's administrative complaint process
- [ ] Obtain the Commissioner's written consent to sue
- [ ] Submit the dispute to appraisal

**§541.154(c)** waives the notice where it is impracticable because the action
must be filed to beat the limitations period, or where the claim is asserted as a
**counterclaim**.

### A producer tells a client a competitor's insurer is financially weak. The statement is false and is made to win the account. Which section, and what is required?

- [x] §541.053, which requires both a false or derogatory statement about financial condition and an intent to injure
- [ ] §541.052, which requires only that the statement be misleading
- [ ] §541.054, which requires concerted action
- [ ] §541.051, which requires a misrepresentation of policy terms

**Both limbs are required.** A true statement about a weak insurer is not
defamation under this section; neither is an idle false one made with no intent
to injure.

### Two agencies agree to refuse to place business with an insurer until it raises commissions. Which section?

- [x] §541.054, boycott, coercion or intimidation
- [ ] §541.053, defamation of an insurer
- [ ] §541.055, false financial statement
- [ ] §541.060, unfair settlement practices

Note **"concerted action or an agreement"** — this is an antitrust provision, and
**one person acting alone is generally outside it**.

### Does §541.052 reach a misleading post on social media?

- [x] Yes — the list covers the Internet and "in any other manner"
- [ ] No, it covers only print, radio and television
- [ ] Only where the post is a paid advertisement
- [ ] Only where the insurer rather than the agent published it

The list was updated in 2007 to name the Internet expressly, and the catch-all
closes the gap for whatever comes next.

### Does Chapter 541 reach an agent personally, or only the insurer?

- [x] Both — §541.002(2) defines "person" to include an agent, broker or adjuster
- [ ] Only the insurer, which is answerable for its agents
- [ ] Only the agent, where the agent acted outside instructions
- [ ] Only licensed entities, not individuals

**§541.008** also directs that the chapter be **liberally construed** to promote
its purposes, so where a provision is capable of two readings the
consumer-protective one is intended.

### A producer induces a client to surrender an existing policy by misrepresenting it. Which two provisions reach that?

- [x] §541.051(5) and §4005.101(b)(7) — it is twisting
- [ ] §541.053 and §4005.101(b)(5)
- [ ] §541.060 and §4005.101(b)(9)
- [ ] §541.059 and §4005.101(b)(8)

Twisting appears both as a **prohibited practice** in Chapter 541 and as a
**ground for discipline** in the licensing chapter.

### Where is rebating prohibited in the Insurance Code?

- [x] §1806.104 for casualty, §1806.053 for automobile and §1806.153 for fire
- [ ] §541.056
- [ ] §544.002
- [ ] §4005.101(b)(9) only

**§541.056 does not exist** — Chapter 541 runs .051 to .055 and then jumps to
.059, the intervening sections having been repealed. Rebating is also a ground
for discipline under §4005.101(b)(9).
