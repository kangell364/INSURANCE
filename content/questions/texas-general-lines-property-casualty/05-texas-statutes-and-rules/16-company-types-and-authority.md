### A policyholder of a mutual insurance company is best described as what?

- [x] An owner of the company, with a vote and a share of surplus
- [ ] A customer of the company, like any other policyholder
- [ ] A shareholder entitled to a share of the company's profit
- [ ] A subscriber who insures the other policyholders directly

**A mutual is owned by its policyholders**, who are its members. Holding the
policy is what makes them an owner — that is the whole distinction from a
stock company, where a policyholder is only a customer and the owners are
shareholders who may hold no policy at all.

"Shareholder" is wrong because a mutual has no shares, and its **policy
dividend is a return of surplus to people who overpaid**, not a distribution
of profit. The last option describes a **reciprocal**, where subscribers
insure each other through an attorney in fact.

### In a reciprocal or interinsurance exchange, who bears the risk?

- [x] The subscribers, who insure one another
- [ ] The attorney in fact, who assumes it on their behalf
- [ ] Shareholders of the exchange
- [ ] Individual underwriters, each for their own share

**The subscribers insure each other.** A reciprocal is not a corporation: it
is an unincorporated association where every member is both insured and
insurer of the others. The **attorney in fact administers** it — acting for
the subscribers under a power of attorney — but does not carry the risk.

The last option describes a **Lloyd's plan**, where individual underwriters
each back their own share. Texas lists both as insurers in **§801.001(2)**,
which is why they are easy to confuse.

### An insurer organised in Louisiana writes in Texas. Domestic, foreign or alien?

- [x] Foreign
- [ ] Domestic
- [ ] Alien
- [ ] Non-admitted

**Foreign means another state.** The trap is that "foreign" sounds like it should
mean overseas; in insurance, overseas is **alien**.

### An insurer organised in Bermuda writes in Texas. Which of the three?

- [x] Alien
- [ ] Foreign
- [ ] Domestic
- [ ] Admitted

**§982.001(2)** — an alien insurance company is one **organised under the laws of
a foreign country.**

### Does holding a certificate of authority tell you where an insurer was organised?

- [x] No — the certificate is about permission to do business in Texas, not origin
- [ ] Yes, only domestic insurers hold certificates
- [ ] Yes, only domestic and foreign insurers hold certificates
- [ ] Yes, the certificate states the state of organisation

**The two questions are independent.** A foreign insurer can be admitted, and a
domestic one can lose its authority.

### When does a certificate of authority expire?

- [x] It does not — it is effective until suspended or revoked
- [ ] Annually, on 1 March
- [ ] Every two years, like an agent's licence
- [ ] Every four years

**§801.053.** This is the opposite of an agent's licence, which is exactly why it
is asked.

### Two applications are equal on the merits, one from a domestic company and one from a foreign company. What does §801.054 require?

- [x] Preference to the domestic company's application
- [ ] Preference to whichever applied first
- [ ] No preference; the Department must treat them identically
- [ ] Preference to the applicant with the larger surplus

The preference is express and unusual, which makes it examinable.

### A client's insurer is non-admitted and becomes insolvent. Is the guaranty association available?

- [x] No — §462.201(2) requires a policy issued by an insurer authorized to engage in business in this state
- [ ] Yes, up to $300,000
- [ ] Yes, but only for workers' compensation claims
- [ ] Yes, if the insurer was an eligible surplus lines insurer

Which is why **§981.101(b)** makes the surplus lines document warn, in 11-point
type, that the insurer is **not a member of the guaranty association**.

### What must a certificate of authority state?

- [x] The specific kinds of insurance authorized under it
- [ ] The insurer's surplus and reserves
- [ ] The territories in which the insurer may write
- [ ] The agents appointed by the insurer

**§801.052.** An insurer authorised for property is not thereby authorised for
life.

### What is an attorney in fact, and which insurers use one?

- [x] Someone authorized under a power of attorney to act for the underwriters — used by a Lloyd's plan and by a reciprocal or interinsurance exchange
- [ ] The insurer's general counsel, used by stock companies
- [ ] A licensed adjuster acting for the insurer
- [ ] The Department's representative in a receivership

**§941.001(2).** It is why the Lloyd's plan and the reciprocal sit next to each
other in every statutory list of insurer types.

### What is a Texas Lloyd's plan?

- [x] A Texas entity under Chapter 941 whose individual underwriters write through an attorney in fact
- [ ] A branch office of Lloyd's of London operating in Texas
- [ ] A reinsurance pool operated by the Department
- [ ] A residual market for commercial property

**Confusing it with Lloyd's of London is the standard error.** The structure is
the same idea — individual underwriters acting through an attorney in fact — but
the entity is a Texan one.

### Under §941.001(4), who may be an "underwriter" on a Lloyd's plan?

- [x] An individual, partnership, or association of individuals
- [ ] Only a capital stock insurance company
- [ ] Only a mutual insurance company
- [ ] Only a reciprocal exchange

The definition is deliberately about **individuals**, which is what distinguishes
the Lloyd's structure from a corporate insurer.

### By when must a foreign insurance company file its annual statement with the Department?

- [x] Not later than 1 March each year
- [ ] Not later than 1 January each year
- [ ] Within 90 days of its fiscal year end
- [ ] Only on the Department's request

**§982.004(a)**, and an authorized **alien** company files its financial statement
by the same date under §982.004(b). **§801.057** makes failure to file a ground
for revocation or suspension.

### What does Chapter 547 govern?

- [x] False advertising by unauthorized insurers, with notice of a violation given to the insurer's domiciliary state
- [ ] The formation of stock and mutual companies
- [ ] Certificates of authority for domestic insurers
- [ ] Adjuster licensing

**§547.052** is the mechanism worth noting: notifying the **domiciliary state** is
how a regulator reaches an insurer it does not license. It is also one of the
chapters an adjuster's CE must cover under §4004.105.

### Which chapter governs unauthorised insurers generally, and which power reaches them urgently?

- [x] Chapter 101, and the emergency cease and desist power in §83.051
- [ ] Chapter 981, and the ordinary cease and desist power in §541.108
- [ ] Chapter 801, and licence revocation under §82.051
- [ ] Chapter 462, and guaranty association assessment

**§83.051** reaches an **unauthorized person** engaging in the business of
insurance in violation of Chapter 101 — and does so **ex parte**, without a prior
hearing.
