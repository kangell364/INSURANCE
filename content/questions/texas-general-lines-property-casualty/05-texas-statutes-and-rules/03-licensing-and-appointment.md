### An agent owes a client a return premium, does not spend it, but has not forwarded it eight months later. Is that a ground for discipline?

- [x] Yes — §4005.101(b)(4) reaches money illegally *withheld*, not only money taken
- [ ] No, because the money was never converted to the agent's own use
- [ ] No, unless the client files a complaint with the Department
- [ ] Only if the amount exceeds a threshold set by the Department

**The subsection names three things: misappropriated, converted to the
licence holder's own use, or illegally withheld.** Withholding is enough on
its own, and it is the one that catches otherwise honest agents.

The money need never be spent. It is not the agent's, and holding it is the
violation — which is why premium is kept where it can be accounted for, and
why **§4001.255** requires insurance records separate from any other business
the agent runs.

### A producer's fiduciary duty to an applicant arises from what?

- [x] Holding money and information that belong to somebody else
- [ ] A fiduciary bond the producer must purchase before licensing
- [ ] The appointment filed by the insurer
- [ ] The agency agreement's express terms

**The duty comes from the position, not from a document.** A producer receives
premium that belongs to the insurer — or back to the applicant if no policy
issues — and receives disclosures made in order to obtain coverage. Holding
either for somebody else is what makes the relationship fiduciary.

**A fiduciary bond is a different thing entirely**: a bond guaranteeing that a
court-appointed executor, guardian or trustee acts faithfully. That is a
product you sell; this is a duty you owe. The exam uses both senses of the
word.

### A newly licensed agent has not yet been appointed by any insurer. May they write business?

- [x] No — §4001.201 requires an appointment by an authorised insurer first
- [ ] Yes, the licence alone permits the sale of any insurer's products
- [ ] Yes, for 30 days after the licence issues
- [ ] Yes, for personal lines but not commercial lines

**The licence says the state permits you to sell insurance. The appointment says
a particular insurer permits you to sell theirs.** You need both.

### An agent takes on a second insurer. By when must the Department be told?

- [x] Not later than the 30th day after the effective date of the appointment
- [ ] Not later than the 14th day
- [ ] Within 60 days
- [ ] At the next licence renewal

**§4001.202(a)** permits an agent to represent **more than one insurer** except
where the code specifically prohibits it. **§4001.202(b)** sets the 30-day
notice, on the prescribed form, with a non-refundable fee for each appointment.

### An applicant wants a licence mainly to write insurance on buildings they own and on their brother's business. Permitted?

- [x] No — §4001.104 bars licensing somebody principally to handle business they control
- [ ] Yes, an applicant may always insure property they own
- [ ] Yes, provided the commission is waived on family business
- [ ] Yes, provided the applicant also solicits some public business

**Insuring your own house is fine. Getting licensed so that you can collect the
commission on your own house, your relatives' houses and your employer's fleet is
not.** The test is whether the controlled business is the **principal** purpose.

### What two aims does §4001.104(b) state?

- [x] To prohibit coercion of insurance, and to preserve each individual's right to choose their own agent or insurer
- [ ] To limit the number of licences issued, and to protect established agencies
- [ ] To ensure adequate examination standards, and to fund the Department
- [ ] To prevent unauthorised insurers from operating in Texas

The subchapter expressly does **not** prohibit an applicant from insuring
property they own or have an interest in. What it prohibits is licensing somebody
to engage in the business **principally to handle business the applicant controls
only through ownership, mortgage, sale, family relationship, or employment**.

### Does a temporary licence holder have to pass the examination first?

- [x] No — §4001.152 requires no written examination
- [ ] Yes, the same examination as a permanent licence
- [ ] Yes, but a shortened version
- [ ] Only for property and casualty lines

**That is the point of a temporary licence**: it lets somebody start while they
prepare for the exam.

### A temporary licence holder sells a policy to somebody who employed them four months ago. May they take the commission?

- [x] No — the bar reaches a person who is, or has been within the past six months, their employer
- [ ] Yes, once the employment has ended
- [ ] Yes, provided the sale was unsolicited
- [ ] Yes, at a reduced commission rate

**The six-month look-back on a former employer** is the detail most likely to be
asked. §4001.157(b) also bars commissions on contracts covering the licence
holder themselves and persons **related by consanguinity or affinity**.

### An agent is convicted of a felony unrelated to insurance. Must the Department be told?

- [x] Yes — §4001.252(a)(2), on a monthly basis, whatever the felony was
- [ ] No, only insurance-related felonies are reportable
- [ ] Yes, but only at the next licence renewal
- [ ] Only if the conviction results in imprisonment

The monthly notification duty covers **a change of mailing address, a felony
conviction, and an administrative action** by a financial or insurance regulator
of Texas, another state, or the United States.

### An agent also runs a tax practice. What does §4001.255 require?

- [x] All insurance records, including customer complaint records, kept separate from the other business
- [ ] A separate licence for each business
- [ ] Written disclosure to clients of the dual occupation
- [ ] Annual reporting of the other business's revenue to the Department

It sounds administrative and is not: the Department can examine the insurance
files **without reaching into an unrelated business**, and premium money stays
traceable.

### How long is a temporary agent's licence valid?

- [x] 180 days after issuance
- [ ] 90 days after issuance
- [ ] One year after issuance
- [ ] Until the applicant passes the examination

**§4001.155** sets 180 days. The Pearson VUE handbook also describes it as not
renewable; the subsection restricting renewal was repealed effective 1 September
2021, so answer 180 days with confidence and treat "not renewable" as what the
handbook says.

### The application, fee and certificate for a temporary licence were mailed nine days ago and nothing has arrived. What may the appointing agent do?

- [x] Assume the licence will be issued, since no denial was notified by the eighth day
- [ ] Nothing until the licence physically arrives
- [ ] Permit the applicant to write business under the appointing agent's own licence
- [ ] Reapply with a second fee

**§4001.154** allows that assumption from the eighth day, provided no denial has
been notified. **§4001.153(a)** requires the Department to issue the licence
**immediately** on receipt of a properly completed application, the
non-refundable fee, and a certificate from the prospective appointing agent,
insurer or HMO.

### To whom may a temporary agent's licence be issued under §4001.151?

- [x] An applicant who is being considered for appointment by an agent, an insurer or an HMO
- [ ] Any applicant who has filed for the examination
- [ ] Any applicant holding a licence in another state
- [ ] Only an applicant replacing a deceased agent

**§4001.156(b)** bars issuance to somebody who does not intend to apply for a
licence to sell to the general public — the same principle as §4001.104.

### Which licence type is governed by Chapter 4101?

- [x] Adjuster
- [ ] Non-resident agent
- [ ] Risk manager
- [ ] Surplus lines agent

Chapters **4001** and **4051** cover agent and agency licensing and property and
casualty agents. Non-resident is **Ch. 4056**, surplus lines **§981.202**, risk
manager **Ch. 4153**, limited **§4051.101** and emergency **§4051.054**.
