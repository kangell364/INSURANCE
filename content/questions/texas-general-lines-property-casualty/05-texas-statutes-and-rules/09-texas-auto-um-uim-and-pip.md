### A Texas insurer issues an auto policy with no UM coverage and no paperwork about it. Is that permitted?

- [x] No — §1952.101(b) requires the coverage unless a named insured rejected it in writing
- [ ] Yes, UM is optional and the insured simply did not buy it
- [ ] Yes, provided the insured was told orally
- [ ] Yes, for policies written through TAIPA

**Mandatory offer, written rejection.** The rejection must exist, and the
requirement applies to TAIPA policies too.

### One of two named insureds signs a written UM rejection. Is the coverage removed?

- [x] Yes — §1952.101(c) says the requirement does not apply if any insured named in the policy rejects it in writing
- [ ] No, all named insureds must sign
- [ ] No, the first named insured must sign
- [ ] Only for the rejecting insured's own injuries

**"Any insured named in the policy"** — one named insured's written rejection is
enough.

### That policy renews the following year. Must the insurer offer UM again?

- [x] No — the rejection carries forward until the named insured requests the coverage in writing
- [ ] Yes, the offer must be renewed each policy term
- [ ] Yes, but only every third year
- [ ] Yes, unless the insured signs a fresh rejection

The carry-forward reaches reinstated and renewal policies, and policies
previously issued **by the same insurer or an affiliated insurer**.

### The insured's UM property damage claim is $4,000. What is paid, before limits?

- [x] $3,750
- [ ] $4,000
- [ ] $3,500
- [ ] Nothing; UM property damage is not available in Texas

**§1952.105(b)** applies a **$250 deductible to UM property damage only**. There
is no deductible on UM bodily injury.

### An insured causes a crash and is injured, and has health insurance. Does PIP pay?

- [x] Yes — PIP is payable without regard to fault and without regard to any collateral source
- [ ] No, the insured was at fault
- [ ] No, the health insurer pays first
- [ ] Only the portion the health insurer declines

**§1952.155(a).** PIP pays even where the insured caused the crash, and pays **on
top of** health insurance rather than behind it.

### A passenger in the insured's car — a friend, not a household member — is injured. Does PIP cover them?

- [x] Yes — PIP covers any authorized operator or passenger, including a guest occupant
- [ ] No, only the named insured and household members
- [ ] Only if the friend has no coverage of their own
- [ ] Only for medical expenses, not lost income

Three features distinguish PIP from ordinary medical payments coverage: it covers
**guest occupants**, it runs for **three years**, and it pays **lost income**.

### The at-fault driver had no insurance at all. May the PIP insurer recover what it paid from them?

- [x] Yes — the no-subrogation rule does not protect a person who had not established financial responsibility
- [ ] No, a PIP insurer never has subrogation rights
- [ ] Only up to the $2,500 statutory figure
- [ ] Only with the insured's written consent

**§1952.155(c)** is the pointed exception to §1952.155(b). **An uninsured driver
gets no protection from the no-subrogation rule.**

### The at-fault driver's insurer is insolvent. Which coverage responds?

- [x] Uninsured motorist — an insolvent liability insurer makes the vehicle uninsured
- [ ] Underinsured motorist
- [ ] Personal injury protection only
- [ ] The Texas guaranty association, exclusively

**§1952.102(a)** includes within "uninsured motor vehicle" an insured vehicle
whose liability insurer **cannot pay within its limits because of insolvency**.

### What is the most PIP an insurer can be compelled to offer?

- [x] $2,500 for all benefits in the aggregate for each person
- [ ] $5,000 per person
- [ ] $2,500 per accident regardless of the number of people
- [ ] $10,000 per person

**§1952.153.** Note the shape: **$2,500 is the maximum an insurer can be compelled
to offer, not a cap on what may be bought.** Higher limits are available; the
statute simply does not require them.

### A driver's policy had adequate limits, but other claimants from the same accident have drawn it down below the insured's own UIM limit. Is the vehicle underinsured?

- [x] Yes — §1952.103 reaches limits reduced by payment of claims arising from the same accident
- [ ] No, underinsured status is fixed at the limits originally written
- [ ] No, once the limits exceeded the statutory minimum
- [ ] Only where the insurer consents to the characterisation

**A policy with adequate limits can become underinsured during the same
accident**, once other claimants have drawn it down.

### May UM bodily injury limits exceed the insured's own liability limits?

- [x] No — they are offered in the amounts the insured desires, but not greater than the policy's bodily injury liability limits
- [ ] Yes, up to twice the liability limits
- [ ] Yes, there is no relationship between the two
- [ ] Only where the insured rejects PIP in writing

**§1952.105(a)**, and the same ceiling applies to UM property damage. Neither may
be offered in amounts **less than** those prescribed by Chapter 601,
Transportation Code.

### Within what period must PIP expenses be incurred?

- [x] Not later than the third anniversary of the accident
- [ ] Not later than the first anniversary
- [ ] Not later than the second anniversary
- [ ] There is no time limit

**§1952.151** also lists what qualifies: necessary medical, surgical, x-ray or
dental services including prosthetic devices, and necessary ambulance, hospital,
professional nursing or funeral services — or, **for an income producer,
replacement of lost income**.

### What does §1952.104(1) prevent?

- [x] Stacking within a single policy — the aggregate to any one person for a single occurrence may not exceed the stated limit
- [ ] An insured from carrying UM with more than one insurer
- [ ] An insurer from writing UM above the statutory minimum
- [ ] A claimant from pursuing both UM and PIP on the same accident

Whatever the number of insureds, policies, vehicles or claims, the total to one
person for one occurrence is capped at the policy's stated limit.

### Chapter 601 of the Transportation Code sets a floor of 30/60/25. Which coverage may not be written below it?

- [x] Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage
- [ ] Personal injury protection
- [ ] Collision coverage
- [ ] Medical payments coverage

**§1952.105(c)** is a **Subchapter C** section, and Subchapter C is uninsured
and underinsured motorist coverage. That is what the Chapter 601 floor
reaches.

**PIP is the trap.** It sits in **Subchapter D**, where §1952.153 caps the
amount an insurer must offer at **$2,500** per person — a ceiling on the
requirement, not a floor tied to Chapter 601. Collision and medical payments
are optional coverages with no statutory minimum at all.
