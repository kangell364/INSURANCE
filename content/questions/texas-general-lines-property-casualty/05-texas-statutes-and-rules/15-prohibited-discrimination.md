### An insurer charges 19-year-old drivers more than 40-year-old drivers, on loss data. Lawful?

- [x] Yes — a distinction based on sound underwriting or actuarial principles is a defence for age
- [ ] No, age is a prohibited characteristic with no exception
- [ ] Only if the difference is under 25 percent
- [ ] Only with the Commissioner's prior approval

Age sits in **§544.002(a)(2)**, and **§544.003(b)** permits a refusal, limitation
or charge **based on sound underwriting or actuarial principles reasonably
related to actual or anticipated loss experience.**

### An insurer declines an applicant because of their national origin, citing loss statistics. Lawful?

- [x] No — the actuarial defence does not reach race, colour, religion or national origin
- [ ] Yes, if the statistics are actuarially sound
- [ ] Yes, provided the insurer applies the rule uniformly
- [ ] Yes, if the Department has approved the underwriting guideline

**§544.003(b)** extends only to **groups (2) and (3)** of §544.002(a). **Group
(1) — race, colour, religion, national origin — is prohibited outright**, and no
loss experience justifies it. That asymmetry is the most examinable thing in the
chapter.

### Two houses on the same street, same construction, same protection class and same claims history are charged different homeowners premiums. Which section is in play?

- [x] §544.052 — unfair discrimination between individuals of the same class and essentially the same hazard
- [ ] §544.002, which lists prohibited characteristics
- [ ] §544.303, on mold underwriting
- [ ] §544.553, on consumer inquiries

**"Same class and essentially the same hazard"** is the operative phrase.
Charging two identical risks differently is unfair discrimination; charging two
different risks differently is underwriting.

### Where must a private action for unfair discrimination under §544.052 be brought?

- [x] A Travis County district court
- [ ] The claimant's county of residence
- [ ] The county where the policy was issued
- [ ] Any district court in Texas

**§544.054(a)** restricts venue to **Travis County**, where the Department sits.
The restriction is unusual, which is exactly why it is asked.

### Within what period must such an action be commenced?

- [x] Two years from the denial or unfair act, or from when it should reasonably have been discovered
- [ ] One year from the denial
- [ ] Four years from the denial
- [ ] Two years from the denial only, with no discovery rule

**§544.054(b)** — the same two-year, discovery-extended shape as Chapter 541.

### A homeowner had mold damage, had it professionally remediated, and holds the certificate. A new insurer declines them for the prior mold claim. Lawful?

- [x] No — §544.303 bars an underwriting decision based on remediated mold damage
- [ ] Yes, prior claims are always valid underwriting information
- [ ] Yes, but only for the first three years after remediation
- [ ] Yes, if the insurer declines all mold claimants uniformly

The certificate of mold remediation under **§1958.154, Occupations Code** is what
establishes it. **A remediated problem is not a live hazard**, and penalising the
owner for fixing it discourages fixing it.

### A policyholder rings to ask whether a cracked window would be covered, does not claim, and finds their renewal premium raised for that reason. Lawful?

- [x] No — §544.553 bars underwriting or rating based solely on a consumer inquiry
- [ ] Yes, any contact about a potential loss is underwriting information
- [ ] Yes, provided the increase is under 10 percent
- [ ] Yes, because no claim was actually filed

The provision exists so a policyholder can **ask a question without it counting
against them**. An inquiry is not a claim and cannot be treated as one.

### Which prevailing-plaintiff remedies does §544.054(c) allow?

- [x] Economic damages, court costs and attorney's fees, and an order enjoining the violation
- [ ] Treble damages and attorney's fees
- [ ] Economic damages only
- [ ] Punitive damages and an injunction

**§544.054(d)** adds that court costs may include **reasonable and necessary
expert witness fees**. Treble damages belong to **Chapter 541**, not here.

### An insurer writes only for members of a professional association. Does that violate §544.002?

- [x] No, provided the membership requirement is uniform, applied uniformly statewide, and qualified members are not otherwise discriminated against
- [ ] Yes, restricting coverage to a group is always prohibited
- [ ] Yes, unless the Department approves the arrangement
- [ ] No, group restrictions are entirely unregulated

**§544.003(a).** The conditions are what keep a genuine affinity programme apart
from a proxy for a prohibited characteristic.

### Which of these does §544.002(a) expressly permit?

- [x] Considering marital status in defining who is eligible for dependent benefits
- [ ] Charging a different rate by religion where loss data supports it
- [ ] Declining an applicant by race where the underwriter documents a reason
- [ ] Limiting coverage by national origin in a defined territory

**§544.002(b).** **§544.002(c)** separately preserves title insurance
requirements relating to community, homestead or other marital rights in land.

### What must an insurer do with its water damage underwriting guidelines?

- [x] File them with the Department, and use only guidelines that accord with the commissioner's rules
- [ ] Publish them to applicants on request
- [ ] Obtain the commissioner's written approval of each guideline
- [ ] Nothing; underwriting guidelines are proprietary

**§544.353(a)–(b).** Subsection (c) separately restricts use of a **prior
appliance-related claim**.

### Where a subchapter of Chapter 544 says conduct is an unfair or deceptive act or practice, what follows?

- [x] It routes back into Chapter 541 and its remedies
- [ ] It becomes a criminal offence automatically
- [ ] It triggers an automatic licence revocation
- [ ] It limits the claimant to administrative relief

**§544.158, §544.203 and §544.254** all do this, which is how a discrimination
violation reaches Chapter 541's private action and treble damages.
