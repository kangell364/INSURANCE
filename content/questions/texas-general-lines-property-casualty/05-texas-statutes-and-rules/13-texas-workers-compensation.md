### A private Texas employer decides not to carry workers' compensation. Is that lawful?

- [x] Yes — §406.002(a) makes coverage elective except for public employers
- [ ] No, coverage is compulsory for every employer with employees
- [ ] Only for employers with fewer than five employees
- [ ] Only with the division's written approval

**Texas is the exception among the states.** Public employers are outside the
election; a private employer chooses, and one that does not elect is a
**non-subscriber**.

### An employee of a non-subscriber is injured partly through their own carelessness and partly through a supervisor's. Which defences are gone?

- [x] Contributory negligence, assumption of risk and the fellow servant rule
- [ ] All defences; the employer is strictly liable
- [ ] Only contributory negligence
- [ ] None — a non-subscriber keeps every common law defence

**§406.033(a).** The non-subscriber surrenders the same three defences the
ordinary bargain takes away — **without getting exclusive remedy in return.**

### On those facts, what must the employee still prove?

- [x] Negligence of the employer, or of an agent or servant acting within the scope of employment
- [ ] Only that the injury occurred at work
- [ ] That the employer acted intentionally
- [ ] Nothing; liability is automatic once the injury is shown

**§406.033(d).** **A non-subscriber is not strictly liable.** No negligence, no
recovery — which is very different from the no-fault system it opted out of, and
the point candidates most often overstate.

### The non-subscriber shows the employee was drunk at the time of the injury. Does that help?

- [x] Yes — intoxication is one of two surviving defences
- [ ] No, all defences were surrendered
- [ ] Only to reduce damages proportionally
- [ ] Only if the employer had a written drug policy

**§406.033(c)** preserves two: that the injury was caused **by an act of the
employee intended to bring about the injury**, or **while the employee was
intoxicated**.

### A non-subscriber asks every new hire to sign away the right to sue for workplace injury. Is that effective?

- [x] No — a pre-injury waiver is void and unenforceable
- [ ] Yes, if signed voluntarily and for consideration
- [ ] Yes, if the employee is told what they are giving up
- [ ] Yes, for at-will employees only

**§406.033(e).** The shape to hold: **pre-injury waiver void; a post-injury
settlement is a different matter.**

### An employee with a $1,000 average weekly wage reaches maximum medical improvement with a 20 percent impairment rating. How long do impairment income benefits run, and at what weekly amount?

- [x] 60 weeks at $700
- [ ] 20 weeks at $700
- [ ] 60 weeks at $750
- [ ] 100 weeks at $700

**Three weeks per percentage point** (§408.121(a)) — 20 × 3 = 60 weeks — at **70
percent of the average weekly wage** (§408.126).

### What two conditions open supplemental income benefits?

- [x] An impairment rating of 15 percent or more, and not returning to work or earning less than 80 percent of the average weekly wage as a direct result
- [ ] An impairment rating of 10 percent or more, and any reduction in earnings
- [ ] Permanent total disability, and exhaustion of temporary benefits
- [ ] An impairment rating of 15 percent or more, and election to commute the impairment benefit

**§408.142(a).** Electing to **commute** part of the impairment income benefit
**disqualifies** the employee, which is why the fourth option is wrong rather than
merely incomplete.

### An employee loses the total and permanent use of both hands. Which benefit, at what rate?

- [x] Lifetime income benefits, at 75 percent of the average weekly wage with a three percent annual increase
- [ ] Impairment income benefits, at 70 percent
- [ ] Supplemental income benefits, at 80 percent
- [ ] Temporary income benefits, at 75 percent

**§408.161(b)** — **total and permanent loss of use of a body part is the loss of
that body part.** LIBs are the only income benefit with a built-in escalator.

### A covered employee dies. What is paid for burial?

- [x] The lesser of the actual reasonable burial expenses or $10,000
- [ ] A flat $10,000
- [ ] The actual expenses, without limit
- [ ] 75 percent of the average weekly wage for 52 weeks

**§408.186(a).** Where the employee died away from their usual place of
employment, the carrier also pays the reasonable cost of **transporting the
body**, capped at the cost of transport to that usual place.

### What is the death benefit rate?

- [x] 75 percent of the average weekly wage
- [ ] 70 percent of the average weekly wage
- [ ] 100 percent of the average weekly wage
- [ ] 66 and two-thirds percent of the average weekly wage

**§408.181(b).** The hook: **70 percent for the two benefits about recovering
(temporary and impairment); 75 percent for the two about not recovering (lifetime
and death).**

### How long do temporary income benefits continue?

- [x] Until the employee reaches maximum medical improvement
- [ ] For 104 weeks from the date of injury
- [ ] Until the impairment rating is certified at 15 percent or more
- [ ] Until the employee returns to any employment

**§408.102(a).** MMI is the hinge the whole benefit structure turns on —
impairment income benefits begin **the day after** it.

### An injured employee earns less than $10 an hour. What changes about temporary income benefits?

- [x] The rate is 75 percent rather than 70 percent for the first 26 weeks
- [ ] The rate is 75 percent for the whole period
- [ ] The benefit is capped at actual earnings with no percentage applied
- [ ] Nothing; the rate is the same for all wage levels

**§408.103(a)(2).** The benefit is measured against the **average weekly wage less
post-injury earnings**, not the wage alone.

### How may a Texas employer obtain workers' compensation coverage?

- [x] Through a licensed insurance company or through self-insurance
- [ ] Only through a licensed insurance company
- [ ] Only through the state fund
- [ ] Through any surplus lines insurer

**§406.003.** The employer must then **notify the division** (§406.004) and
**notify its employees** (§406.005) — and failing to notify employees is an
**administrative violation**.

### Which code holds Texas workers' compensation law?

- [x] Title 5 of the Labor Code
- [ ] Title 5 of the Insurance Code
- [ ] The Transportation Code
- [ ] The Business & Commerce Code

Which is why **§31.002(2)** makes administering the workers' compensation system a
statutory duty of the Texas Department of Insurance, and why the exam reaches
outside the Insurance Code for it.
