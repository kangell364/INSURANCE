### A building is worth $500,000. The policy carries an 80% coinsurance clause, a $300,000 limit and a $1,000 deductible. Fire causes $100,000 of damage. What does the policy pay?

- [ ] $100,000
- [x] $74,000
- [ ] $75,000
- [ ] $79,000

Coinsurance is applied first, then the deductible. Should = 80% × $500,000 =
$400,000. Did = $300,000. 300 ÷ 400 = 0.75. 0.75 × $100,000 = $75,000, less
the $1,000 deductible = **$74,000**. Answering $75,000 means the deductible was
forgotten; $79,000 means it was subtracted from the loss before the fraction
was applied.

### A building worth $800,000 carries 80% coinsurance and a $500,000 limit. A $200,000 loss occurs, with a $2,500 deductible. What is paid?

- [ ] $200,000
- [ ] $125,000
- [x] $153,750
- [ ] $156,250

Should = 80% × $800,000 = $640,000. Did = $500,000. 500 ÷ 640 = 0.78125.
× $200,000 = $156,250, less $2,500 = **$153,750**. $156,250 is the figure
before the deductible.

### An insured carries a limit ABOVE the coinsurance requirement. A partial loss occurs. How does the extra insurance affect the payment?

- [x] It does not increase it — the loss is paid in full, less the deductible, up to the limit
- [ ] It increases the payment proportionally
- [ ] It removes the deductible
- [ ] It converts the settlement to replacement cost

Once the did/should fraction reaches 1 there is nothing left for coinsurance to
reduce. **Insurance never pays more than the loss**, so over-insuring buys
nothing but a larger premium.

### A homeowner has a 2% wind deductible on a dwelling insured for $350,000. Hail causes $12,000 of damage. What is paid?

- [ ] $11,760
- [x] $5,000
- [ ] $12,000
- [ ] $7,000

A percentage deductible is taken on the **insured value**, not on the loss:
2% × $350,000 = $7,000, so $12,000 − $7,000 = **$5,000**. $11,760 is 2% of the
loss deducted instead — the mistake the question is built to catch.

### Under split limits of 250/500/100, three people are injured with bodily injury claims of $200,000, $200,000 and $150,000. What does the policy pay for bodily injury?

- [ ] $550,000
- [x] $500,000
- [ ] $250,000
- [ ] $750,000

Each claim is within the $250,000 per-person limit, so none is individually
capped — but they total $550,000 against a $500,000 per-accident limit, so
**$500,000** is paid. $550,000 ignores the per-accident limit; $250,000 applies
the per-person limit to the total.

### Which deductible is measured in time rather than money?

- [ ] Aggregate
- [ ] Disappearing
- [x] Waiting period
- [ ] Percentage

A **waiting period** deductible requires a stated time to pass before benefits
begin, and is used in business interruption and disability. An aggregate
deductible is a running money total; a disappearing deductible shrinks as the
loss grows.

### Why does coinsurance exist?

- [ ] To discourage small claims
- [x] Because most property losses are partial, so a low limit would buy nearly full protection at a fraction of the premium
- [ ] To reduce the insurer's exposure to catastrophic losses
- [ ] Because replacement cost exceeds actual cash value

Total losses are rare. Somebody insuring a $1,000,000 building for $200,000 is
not buying a fifth of the protection — they are buying most of it. Coinsurance
makes the premium correspond to the exposure. Discouraging small claims is what
a **deductible** does.

### A coinsurance clause requires 80%. At what point is the property's value measured?

- [ ] When the policy was written
- [x] At the time of the loss
- [ ] At the most recent renewal
- [ ] When the coinsurance percentage was selected

Value is measured **at the time of the loss**, which is why a building that
appreciates can leave a once-compliant insured underinsured without anything
about the policy having changed.

### What is the effect of an agreed value endorsement?

- [x] It suspends the coinsurance clause
- [ ] It raises the policy limit to full replacement cost
- [ ] It removes the deductible
- [ ] It guarantees payment of the stated amount on any loss

The insurer has already accepted the property's value, usually on a statement
of values, so there is nothing left for coinsurance to police. It does not
change the limit, the deductible, or turn the policy into a valued contract for
partial losses.

### A policy has a $1,000,000 per-occurrence limit and a $2,000,000 aggregate. Claims of $800,000 and $700,000 have already been paid this period. A third claim of $900,000 arrives. What is paid?

- [ ] $900,000
- [x] $500,000
- [ ] $0
- [ ] $1,000,000

The aggregate is the cap for the whole policy period. $800,000 + $700,000 =
$1,500,000 paid, leaving **$500,000** of the $2,000,000 aggregate. The
per-occurrence limit would have allowed the full $900,000, but the aggregate
runs out first — and once it is exhausted the policy is finished for the
period even though months may remain.
