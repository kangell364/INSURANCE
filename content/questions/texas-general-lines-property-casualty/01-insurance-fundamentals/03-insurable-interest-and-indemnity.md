### When must insurable interest exist in a property policy?

- [ ] At the time the policy is written only
- [x] At the time of the loss
- [ ] Both when the policy is written and at the time of loss
- [ ] Continuously throughout the policy period

**Property insurance requires insurable interest at the time of loss.** Life
insurance is the opposite — interest must exist at inception and need not
continue. Mixing the two up is the most common error on this topic.

### Who has an insurable interest in a mortgaged building?

- [ ] The owner only
- [ ] The mortgagee only
- [x] Both the owner and the mortgagee, to the extent of their interests
- [ ] Whichever party is named first on the declarations

Both stand to lose financially if the building burns, so both have an
interest — the owner in the equity, the lender in the debt. Payment is made to
them jointly, as their interests appear.

### Which of these does NOT create an insurable interest in property?

- [ ] Ownership
- [ ] A secured loan against it
- [ ] A lease of it
- [x] Expecting to inherit it under a will

An expectation of future benefit is not a present financial interest. The
person suffers no loss today if the property is destroyed today.

### What is the principle of indemnity?

- [x] The insured should be restored to their pre-loss financial position, no better
- [ ] The insurer must pay the policy limit on any covered loss
- [ ] The insured must be made whole regardless of the limit
- [ ] Losses must be paid within a set number of days

"No better" is the operative half. Everything from actual cash value to
subrogation to the other-insurance clause exists to enforce it.

### Which provision exists specifically to prevent an insured profiting from a loss?

- [ ] The insuring agreement
- [ ] The cancellation condition
- [x] The other insurance clause
- [ ] The liberalization clause

Where two policies cover one loss, the other-insurance clause divides it so
the insured collects the loss **once**. Subrogation serves the same principle
from the other direction.

### An insured's car worth $18,000 is destroyed. They hold two policies, each with a $20,000 limit. How much can they collect in total?

- [ ] $40,000
- [ ] $20,000
- [x] $18,000
- [ ] $9,000

**The loss, once.** The two insurers divide it between them; the insured is
indemnified, not enriched. Which insurer pays what is the other-insurance
clause's business, not the insured's.

### Which type of policy is an exception to strict indemnity?

- [x] A valued policy, which pays a stated amount without proof of actual value
- [ ] A named peril policy
- [ ] An open peril policy
- [ ] A blanket policy

A **valued** policy pays the agreed figure, which is why it is used for fine
art and antiques where actual cash value would be argued about indefinitely.
Life insurance is the other classic exception.

### What is subrogation?

- [ ] The insurer's right to cancel a policy after a claim
- [x] The insurer's right, after paying a claim, to pursue whoever caused the loss
- [ ] The insured's right to transfer the policy to a buyer
- [ ] The right of two insurers to share a loss

It preserves indemnity — without it the insured could collect from the insurer
*and* sue the wrongdoer, ending up ahead — and it puts the cost on the party at
fault rather than the pool.

### An insured signs a release of the negligent party AFTER a loss, then claims on the policy. What is the likely result?

- [x] Coverage is prejudiced, because the insurer's subrogation right was destroyed
- [ ] The claim is paid in full; releases do not affect coverage
- [ ] The claim is paid, then the insurer sues the insured
- [ ] Coverage continues but the deductible doubles

Signing away recovery **after** a loss defeats the insurer's right and can void
the claim. Signing a hold-harmless **before** a loss — in a lease or a
construction contract — is generally permitted, and that before-and-after
distinction is what gets tested.

### Why is a wagering contract unenforceable as insurance?

- [ ] Because the amounts involved are too small
- [x] Because there is no insurable interest, so the arrangement creates a loss that would not otherwise exist
- [ ] Because the losses are not accidental
- [ ] Because no premium is paid

Insuring something you have no stake in gives you a reason to want it
destroyed. Insurable interest is the requirement that stops insurance becoming
a bet, and it is the "legal purpose" element of contract law doing its work.
