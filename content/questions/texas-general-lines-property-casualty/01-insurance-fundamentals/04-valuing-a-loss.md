### An older home is insured on an HO-8. A damaged section of ornate plaster cornice is destroyed. On what basis is it settled?

- [x] Repair cost, using common modern construction materials
- [ ] Replacement cost, matching the original plasterwork
- [ ] Market value of the whole dwelling
- [ ] Agreed value fixed when the policy was written

**The HO-8 settles on a modified basis — repair cost with common
construction materials**, not a reproduction of what was there. The cornice is
made good with modern materials; nobody hand-casts new plaster.

**This exists to make an older home insurable at all.** For some houses the
cost of reproducing the original craftsmanship far exceeds what the property is
worth, and insuring it for that sum would create a **moral hazard** — the
building would be worth more burnt than standing.

**Functional replacement cost is the same idea one step up**: replacing with
modern materials that serve the same function, rather than repairing what is
damaged. Both settle below true replacement cost and for the same reason.

### Under a replacement-cost policy the insurer first pays actual cash value. What is the withheld amount called, and when is it released?

- [x] Recoverable depreciation, released once the work is actually done
- [ ] Non-recoverable depreciation, released at the insurer's discretion
- [ ] Salvage value, released when the damaged property is surrendered
- [ ] A deductible, which is never released

**Recoverable depreciation is the gap between the ACV payment and the
replacement cost**, and it is released when the insured actually repairs or
replaces.

**Under an ACV policy that same gap is non-recoverable** — never paid, because
actual cash value is all the policy promised. The word that changes is the
policy's valuation basis, not the arithmetic.

This is why a replacement-cost policyholder who takes the first cheque and
never rebuilds ends up with ACV: **the second payment is conditional on the
work, and doing nothing satisfies no condition.**

### How is actual cash value most commonly calculated?

- [x] Replacement cost less depreciation
- [ ] Market value less the deductible
- [ ] Original purchase price less depreciation
- [ ] The amount a willing buyer would pay

**Replacement cost minus depreciation.** Original purchase price is irrelevant —
what matters is what it costs to replace the item today, reduced for the life
it has already had.

### A ten-year-old roof with a twenty-year life is destroyed. Replacing it costs $20,000. What is the actual cash value?

- [ ] $20,000
- [x] $10,000
- [ ] $15,000
- [ ] $5,000

Half the roof's useful life was gone, so half the replacement cost is
depreciated: **$10,000**. Under a replacement cost policy the insured would
receive ACV first and the remaining $10,000 once the roof is actually replaced.

### Under a replacement cost policy, when is the depreciation holdback paid?

- [ ] Immediately with the first payment
- [x] Once the property is actually repaired or replaced
- [ ] Thirty days after the proof of loss
- [ ] Only if the insured requests it in writing

The insurer pays ACV first and the **holdback** on completion. An insured who
takes the cheque and never rebuilds keeps the ACV and nothing more — which is
indemnity working exactly as intended.

### Which valuation method is generally NOT used to settle a property claim?

- [ ] Actual cash value
- [ ] Replacement cost
- [ ] Agreed value
- [x] Market value

**Market value reflects location and demand, not the cost of rebuilding.** A
house in a poor market can cost far more to rebuild than to sell — which is
exactly why the HO-8 form exists for older homes.

### What is stated value?

- [x] An amount the insured declares, which caps the payment but does not guarantee it
- [ ] An amount the insurer guarantees to pay on a total loss
- [ ] The market value at the time of loss
- [ ] Replacement cost adjusted annually for inflation

**Stated value caps; agreed value guarantees.** Under stated value the insurer
still pays the lesser of that figure and the actual loss. That distinction is
the point of the question.

### For which property is an agreed value basis most appropriate?

- [ ] A newly built warehouse
- [x] A nineteenth-century painting
- [ ] A fleet of delivery vans
- [ ] Retail stock

Where actual cash value would be genuinely contestable — fine art, antiques,
collectibles — settling the value in advance avoids an argument at the worst
possible moment.

### What is salvage value?

- [x] What the damaged property is worth in its damaged state
- [ ] The cost of removing debris after a loss
- [ ] The amount recovered from the negligent party
- [ ] The undepreciated portion of a replacement cost claim

Debris removal is a separate additional coverage; recovery from a wrongdoer is
**subrogation**. Salvage is simply what the wreck is worth.

### An insurer pays a total loss in full on a vehicle. Who owns the wreck?

- [x] The insurer
- [ ] The insured
- [ ] Whoever recovers it first
- [ ] It is sold and the proceeds split equally

**Salvage rights follow payment.** Having paid the whole loss, the insurer takes
what is left — the same logic as subrogation, applied to property instead of to
a wrongdoer.

### A functional replacement cost settlement does what?

- [x] Pays to restore the property using modern materials serving the same function, rather than matching obsolete originals
- [ ] Pays actual cash value plus the deductible
- [ ] Pays only for the portion of the building in use
- [ ] Pays market value where it exceeds replacement cost

Replacing lath-and-plaster with drywall restores the function without paying
for craftsmanship the building no longer needs. It underpins the HO-8's
modified settlement basis.

### Why does the HO-8 form exist?

- [ ] To cover mobile homes
- [x] Because for some older homes replacement cost far exceeds market value, making full replacement cost coverage unaffordable and a moral hazard
- [ ] To provide open peril coverage on contents
- [ ] To cover dwellings rented to others

A large Victorian in a low-value neighbourhood might cost three times its sale
price to rebuild. HO-8 settles on a modified repair-cost basis and pairs it
with a narrower peril list.
