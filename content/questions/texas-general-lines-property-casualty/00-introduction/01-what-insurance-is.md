### Which best describes what an insurance contract does for the insured?

- [ ] It prevents the loss from occurring
- [x] It transfers the financial consequence of a loss to the insurer
- [ ] It saves the premium so the insured can meet the loss themselves
- [ ] It guarantees the insured a profit if a loss occurs

**Transfer is what the contract does for the insured.** Something uncertain,
large and unknown becomes something certain, small and known — the premium.

The other three are the three things insurance is routinely mistaken for.
It does not **prevent** losses; that is a different risk-management technique
and insurance answers for the loss afterwards. It is not a **savings
account**; most policyholders collect nothing and that is the system working.
And it never pays a **profit** — restoring the insured and no more is the
principle of indemnity.

### An insurer cannot say whether one particular house will burn, yet prices the policy confidently. Why?

- [ ] Because the premium is set high enough to cover any single loss
- [x] Because it insures many similar exposures, and the aggregate is predictable even though no single one is
- [ ] Because it can cancel the policy if a loss looks likely
- [ ] Because the state sets the rate

**Pooling.** One house is unpredictable; a hundred thousand similar houses are
not. The insurer never needs to know *which* will burn — only roughly how
many.

Keep the pair straight: **transfer** is what the contract does for the
insured, **pooling** is what makes the insurer able to accept it.

### A guest is injured at the insured's home and claims against them. What is the guest, in insurance terms?

- [ ] The first party
- [ ] The second party
- [x] A third party
- [ ] An additional insured

**First party is the insured; second party is the insurer; a third party is
somebody outside the contract with a claim against the insured.**

The numbering carries all the way through the course: property insurance
mostly answers to the first party, liability insurance answers to the third.

An **additional insured** is a different thing — a person brought *inside* the
contract by its terms or an endorsement, not someone claiming against it.

### An insurer accepts thousands of similar risks so that the premiums of the many meet the losses of the few. What is that arrangement called?

- [ ] Transfer
- [x] Pooling
- [ ] Indemnity
- [ ] Reinsurance

**Pooling** — the mechanism that makes an insurer able to accept risks no
individual could carry. Many similar exposures, many premiums, out of which
the few actual losses are paid.

**Transfer** is the other half of the pair and the more commonly chosen wrong
answer. Transfer is what the *contract* does for the insured: it moves the
financial consequence. Pooling is what the *insurer* does with thousands of
those transfers so that it can honour them.

**Indemnity** is the principle that a claim restores and does not enrich.
**Reinsurance** is an insurer transferring risk onward to another insurer —
pooling one level up, but not the word for this.
