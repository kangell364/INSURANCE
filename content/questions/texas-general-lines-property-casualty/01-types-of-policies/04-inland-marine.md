### A photographer owns $30,000 of camera equipment used at client sites. What form fits, and why not the homeowners policy?

- [x] A camera floater — the homeowners policy sub-limits cameras, covers them on a named peril basis away from home, and settles at ACV
- [ ] The homeowners policy, since Coverage C follows property worldwide
- [ ] A dwelling policy with a theft endorsement
- [ ] Commercial general liability

Inland marine gives **open peril coverage, following the property anywhere, at an
agreed value, with no coinsurance**. That combination is why the floater exists.

### A dry cleaner's premises burn, destroying customers' garments. The cleaner was not negligent. Which form pays the customers?

- [x] A bailee's customers form, which pays regardless of negligence
- [ ] A legal liability form, since the cleaner held the goods
- [ ] The commercial property form's personal property of others category
- [ ] None; the customers' own homeowners policies respond

**Bailee's customers coverage pays whether or not the bailee was negligent**,
because arguing about fault with a customer whose suit you burned is bad
business. A **legal liability** form covers only what the bailee is legally
responsible for — cheaper, and much less useful.

### Why does a floater usually have no coinsurance clause?

- [x] Scheduled items carry agreed or stated values the insurer has already accepted
- [ ] Inland marine rates are unregulated
- [ ] Floaters are written for less than one year
- [ ] Coinsurance applies only to buildings

There is nothing left for a coinsurance clause to police once the insurer has
agreed the value in advance.

### A contractor's excavator is damaged while being towed between sites. Which floater?

- [x] Contractor's equipment floater
- [ ] Installation floater
- [ ] Motor truck cargo
- [ ] Builders risk

A **contractor's equipment floater** covers mobile tools, machinery and equipment
on site and in transit. An **installation floater** covers materials from the time
they leave the supplier until installed and accepted.

### A fire destroys a firm's ledgers and it cannot collect $200,000 of debts. Which coverage, and what does it pay?

- [x] Accounts receivable — the uncollectible sums, the cost of reconstructing records, and extra collection expense
- [ ] Valuable papers and records — the market value of the debts
- [ ] Business income — the earnings the debts represented
- [ ] Commercial property — the ACV of the ledgers

**Valuable papers pays for reconstruction** of documents, not for the
information's abstract worth. **Accounts receivable** is the coverage aimed at the
uncollectible debt itself.

### Why is a bridge classed as inland marine?

- [x] It is an instrumentality of transportation, a historical inland marine class
- [ ] It is exposed to water damage
- [ ] Its value cannot be determined at a fixed location
- [ ] It moves under load

Marine underwriters followed cargo off the ship and inland; bridges, tunnels,
pipelines, power lines and radio towers were classed as **instrumentalities of
transportation and communication** and stayed there. It feels wrong until you
know the history.

### What does scheduling a $15,000 necklace on a personal articles floater achieve?

- [x] Removes the homeowners special limit, broadens to open peril, agrees the value and usually drops the deductible
- [ ] Doubles the homeowners theft sub-limit
- [ ] Converts Coverage C settlement to replacement cost for all contents
- [ ] Adds flood and earthquake coverage for the item

All four benefits arrive together, which is why scheduling is the standard advice
for jewellery, furs, cameras, musical instruments, silverware, golfer's
equipment, fine arts and stamp or coin collections.

### How are fine arts normally written, and what exclusion do they typically carry?

- [x] On a valued basis, with a breakage exclusion unless bought back
- [ ] On an actual cash value basis, with a theft exclusion
- [ ] On a replacement cost basis, with a flood exclusion
- [ ] On a blanket basis, with no exclusions

A painting's actual cash value would be argued about for months, so the value is
**agreed in advance**. **Breakage** is the standard carve-out, and it can usually
be bought back.

### An insured buys a ring of a scheduled class and has not yet reported it. Is it covered?

- [x] Usually yes, automatically for a limited period — commonly 30 days — if the insurer is notified and premium paid
- [ ] No, coverage begins only when the item is added to the schedule
- [ ] Yes, permanently, up to the highest scheduled item's limit
- [ ] Only if the item replaces one previously scheduled

**Newly acquired property** of a scheduled class carries automatic coverage for a
short window, which exists precisely so that a purchase is not uninsured on the
drive home.

### What is the purpose of the nationwide marine definition?

- [x] To stop insurers writing ordinary fire business on inland marine forms to escape rate regulation
- [ ] To set uniform inland marine rates across the states
- [ ] To define which waters count as inland
- [ ] To require open peril coverage on all floaters

The definition sets out what may be written as inland marine. **Controlled** lines
are filed classes; **uncontrolled** lines may be written on individually
negotiated terms.

### Which inland marine form typically covers mechanical breakdown and electrical disturbance that standard property forms exclude?

- [x] Electronic data processing
- [ ] Accounts receivable
- [ ] Transportation / trip transit
- [ ] Jewellers block

**EDP** coverage reaches computer hardware, software and data on an open peril
basis and typically includes the breakdown and electrical-disturbance causes the
standard forms carve out.

### A trucking company hauls goods for hire and wants coverage for the cargo it carries. Which form?

- [x] Motor truck cargo
- [ ] Installation floater
- [ ] Contractor's equipment floater
- [ ] Ocean marine cargo

**Motor truck cargo** covers the carrier's liability for the goods it is hauling.
**Ocean marine** — hull, cargo, freight, and protection and indemnity — remains a
separate line on the water.
