### An earthquake cracks a gas line and the resulting fire destroys the house. What does a standard homeowners policy with no earthquake endorsement cover?

- [x] The fire damage — ensuing fire is not excluded by the earth movement exclusion
- [ ] Nothing, because the proximate cause was the earthquake
- [ ] The shake damage only
- [ ] Everything, since fire is a named peril

**Fire following an earthquake is covered.** The earth movement exclusion is
written so that **ensuing fire** falls outside it. The shake damage itself is not
covered without the endorsement.

### A $500,000 dwelling carries earthquake coverage with a 10% deductible. A quake causes $70,000 of damage. What is paid?

- [x] $20,000
- [ ] $63,000
- [ ] $70,000
- [ ] Nothing

**The percentage applies to the limit, not to the loss.** Ten per cent of
$500,000 is a $50,000 deductible, leaving $20,000. Earthquake deductibles
commonly run from 2% to 20% of the limit.

### Why is a mobile home not written on a homeowners policy?

- [x] It is built to different standards, depreciates rather than appreciates, and can be moved
- [ ] Mobile homes carry no insurable interest until permanently affixed
- [ ] Homeowners forms exclude all manufactured construction by statute
- [ ] Mobile homes are covered under auto policies instead

A mobile homeowners policy is structured like a homeowners policy but settles
losses at **actual cash value**, addresses **transportation and permission to
move**, and requires the unit to be of a minimum size, designed for year-round
living and at a fixed location.

### A farmer keeps a herd of cattle, farms 400 acres and lives in a house on the property. What policy?

- [x] Farmowners — a package covering the residence and the farming operation
- [ ] Homeowners, with a farming endorsement
- [ ] A dwelling policy plus a commercial package policy
- [ ] Commercial property with a livestock floater

**A farm is ineligible for a homeowners policy.** Farmowners combines the
dwelling and contents, farm structures, farm personal property including
livestock, and farm liability that reaches the operation and typically farm
employees.

### Cattle are killed by lightning. Covered under a farmowners policy?

- [x] Yes — lightning is a standard peril and electrocution is among the named livestock perils
- [ ] No, livestock is covered only against theft
- [ ] Only if the cattle were scheduled individually
- [ ] Only under a separate crop insurance policy

Livestock is usually written on a **named peril** basis including perils peculiar
to the exposure: **electrocution, attack by dogs or wild animals, accidental
shooting, drowning, and loading and unloading**.

### Why is windstorm excluded on the coast when it is covered inland?

- [x] The exposure is correlated — one hurricane damages an entire coastline at once
- [ ] Coastal construction standards are unregulated
- [ ] Federal law prohibits private wind coverage in coastal counties
- [ ] Wind losses inland are more severe per property

A correlated catastrophe defeats the law of large numbers. Where the private
market withdraws, a **residual market** fills the gap — a windstorm pool or
association, or a **FAIR Plan** for those unable to obtain basic property
coverage in the voluntary market.

### Multiple earthquake shocks hit within 48 hours. How many deductibles apply?

- [x] One — shocks within a stated period, commonly 72 hours, are a single occurrence
- [ ] One per shock
- [ ] One per day on which a shock occurred
- [ ] One per damaged structure

The 72-hour clause exists so that an insured is not deductibled repeatedly for
what is plainly one event.

### Which of these is NOT within the standard earth movement exclusion?

- [x] Mudflow inundating two or more properties
- [ ] Landslide
- [ ] Subsidence
- [ ] Sinkhole collapse

The exclusion is **broader than "earthquake"** — landslide, mudslide, subsidence,
sinkhole collapse and earth sinking, rising or shifting are all within it.
**Mudflow** is within the NFIP's flood definition and belongs there. Buying
earthquake coverage does not necessarily buy every earth-movement peril back.

### How are residual market mechanisms such as a windstorm association funded?

- [x] By compulsory membership of the insurers writing in the state
- [ ] By federal appropriation
- [ ] By a surcharge on all policyholders nationally
- [ ] By the policyholders of the association alone

They are **the market insuring itself**. Insurers writing in the state must
belong, and assessments fall on them.

### What coverage does a yacht policy provide for liability?

- [x] Protection and indemnity, the marine world's liability coverage
- [ ] Commercial general liability
- [ ] Personal liability under Section II of a homeowners form
- [ ] Motor truck cargo liability

A yacht policy follows ocean marine structure: **hull** on the vessel,
**protection and indemnity** for liability — broader than a general liability form
in respect of crew and other maritime exposures — plus medical payments and
coverage for trailer and equipment.

### What is a lay-up period in a watercraft policy?

- [x] A stated time during which the vessel must be out of the water, with coverage restricted outside those terms
- [ ] A waiting period before coverage attaches
- [ ] A period during which claims may not be filed
- [ ] The interval between survey inspections

Alongside **navigational limits**, which specify the waters in which the vessel is
covered, the lay-up period is the way a marine underwriter controls when and
where the exposure exists.

### A homeowner keeps a 24-foot boat with a 200-horsepower outboard. What does the homeowners policy do for it?

- [x] Very little — it covers only small, low-powered craft, on a narrow peril basis, and excludes liability for larger or faster boats
- [ ] Covers it fully under Coverage C, worldwide
- [ ] Covers the hull but not the motor
- [ ] Covers it up to the Coverage B limit

A vessel of that size needs a **boatowners policy** — a package covering hull,
motor, trailer and equipment plus liability and medical payments — or, for larger
vessels, a yacht policy.
