### An applicant states the roof was replaced in 2019. It was 2017, and they genuinely misremembered. What is this?

- [x] A misrepresentation, avoidable only if material and relied on
- [ ] Concealment, because the true date was withheld
- [ ] Fraud, because the statement was false
- [ ] A breach of warranty, voiding the policy automatically

A **representation** is a statement believed true when made. A false one is a
**misrepresentation**, and on its own it costs nothing: the insurer must also
show the falsehood was **material** and that it **relied** on it. A two-year
error in a roof date probably is not.

### An applicant knows the property has an open code violation and says nothing. No question was asked about it. What is this?

- [x] Concealment
- [ ] Misrepresentation
- [ ] A permitted omission, since no question was asked
- [ ] Breach of a promissory warranty

**Concealment is silence** about a material fact the applicant knew and was
obliged to disclose. The three elements are materiality, knowledge and
intentional withholding. Not volunteering a hobby nobody asked about is not
concealment; hiding a condemned building is.

### What distinguishes a warranty from a representation?

- [x] A warranty is guaranteed true; a representation is believed true
- [ ] A warranty is written; a representation may be oral
- [ ] A warranty is made by the insurer; a representation by the insured
- [ ] A warranty applies to commercial policies only

**Representation = believed true. Warranty = guaranteed true.** A warranty
becomes part of the contract, and in strict theory its breach lets the insurer
void the policy **without showing materiality**.

### A commercial policy is issued on a warranty that a burglar alarm will be maintained. What kind of warranty is that?

- [x] A promissory (continuing) warranty
- [ ] An affirmative warranty
- [ ] An implied warranty
- [ ] A representation of future intent

An **affirmative** warranty is a statement of fact **at issue** — "a sprinkler
system is installed". A **promissory** warranty promises something **will remain
true throughout the term** — "the system will be maintained".

### The insured disconnects that alarm and an unrelated fire destroys the building. On strict warranty doctrine, what may the insurer do?

- [x] Void the policy for breach, without showing the breach caused the loss
- [ ] Nothing, because the alarm had no connection to the fire
- [ ] Reduce the claim by the premium credit given for the alarm
- [ ] Cancel prospectively but pay the fire loss

Strict warranty doctrine does not ask about causation. Because that result is
harsh, **many states have modified it by statute** — treating applicant
statements as representations, or requiring materiality or a causal connection.

### An insured inflates a genuine $8,000 theft claim to $20,000. Which of the four is this?

- [x] Fraud
- [ ] Misrepresentation
- [ ] Concealment
- [ ] Breach of warranty

**Claim fraud** — intentional deception to obtain something of value, whether or
not the original application was honest. Fraud is the most serious of the four
and the only one that is also a **crime**.

### Is a property policy subject to an incontestable clause?

- [ ] Yes, after two years the insurer may no longer contest it
- [x] No — incontestability is a life and health provision
- [ ] Yes, but only for misstatements of age
- [ ] Only where the policy has been continuously renewed for five years

P&C policies are **short-term and re-underwritten each term**, so the insurer's
remedy needs no deadline. Where "incontestability" appears as an answer to a P&C
fact pattern, it is usually the distractor.

### What is the effect of a material misrepresentation on a policy?

- [x] It makes the policy voidable at the insurer's option
- [ ] It makes the policy void from inception, automatically
- [ ] It reduces recovery proportionally to the understated premium
- [ ] It has no effect once the first premium has been accepted

**Voidable, not void.** The insurer may rescind, usually returning the premium —
or may choose to keep the policy in force. Fraud, by contrast, makes the policy
**void**.

### Which test determines whether a misstatement is material?

- [x] Whether the insurer would have declined, charged more, or issued different terms had it known the truth
- [ ] Whether the misstatement related to the loss that occurred
- [ ] Whether the misstatement was made deliberately
- [ ] Whether the misstatement exceeded 10 percent of any stated value

Materiality is measured at **underwriting**, not at claim. Deliberateness is what
separates concealment and fraud from misrepresentation; it is not the
materiality test.

### In which line are warranties most commonly used?

- [x] Commercial lines
- [ ] Personal auto
- [ ] Homeowners
- [ ] Personal umbrella

Warranties appear where the insurer prices the premium around a **specific
protective feature the insured must maintain** — sprinklers, alarms, guard
service. That is a commercial pattern far more than a personal one.
