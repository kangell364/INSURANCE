### An insured's car is stolen. What two notifications are required?

- [x] The insurer and the police
- [ ] The insurer and the lienholder
- [ ] The insurer and the state motor vehicle department
- [ ] The insurer only

Theft carries a **second notice duty to the police**. The parallel is credit
card forgery, where the insured must also notify the **card issuer**.

### A storm breaks a window. The insured leaves it open for three weeks and rain ruins the floor. Is the floor damage covered?

- [ ] Yes, the floor damage flows from a covered peril
- [x] No — the insured breached the duty to protect the property from further damage
- [ ] Yes, but reduced by the cost of the repair the insured should have made
- [ ] Only if the insured can show the repair was impossible

Protecting the property is a **duty, not a courtesy**. The neglect exclusion
removes damage the insured allowed to happen by failing to act. Reasonable
emergency repair costs, by contrast, are usually payable — keep the receipts.

### Must a proof of loss be in writing?

- [x] Yes — written, signed and sworn
- [ ] No, an oral statement of the amount is sufficient
- [ ] Only for losses above the deductible
- [ ] Only where the insurer specifically requests one in writing

Notice of claim may usually be **oral and prompt**. Proof of loss is **signed
and sworn**, and is normally due **within 60 days of the insurer's request**.
The exam tests the two against each other constantly.

### A policy has a $500,000 limit. A covered suit costs $120,000 to defend and settles for $500,000. What has the insurer paid in total?

- [ ] $500,000
- [x] $620,000
- [ ] $380,000
- [ ] $120,000

Defence costs are generally paid **in addition to** the limit of liability. The
duty to defend ends when the limit is exhausted by payment of judgments or
settlements — which is why a liability limit is worth more than its face value.

### The insured says a fire loss is $90,000; the insurer says $60,000. Both agree fire is covered. What provision applies?

- [x] Appraisal
- [ ] Arbitration of coverage
- [ ] Subrogation
- [ ] Salvage

Either party may demand **appraisal**. Each selects a competent, independent
appraiser; the two select an **umpire**. Agreement by **any two of the three**
sets the amount.

### A homeowner with replacement cost coverage suffers a $40,000 loss on property with an ACV of $28,000, and does not rebuild. What is paid?

- [ ] $40,000
- [x] $28,000
- [ ] $12,000
- [ ] Nothing, because the property was not replaced

Replacement cost forms pay **actual cash value first** and release the
**holdback** — the depreciation — only when the repair or replacement is
actually complete. An insured who never rebuilds keeps ACV.

### An insurer demands appraisal. Has it waived its right to deny the claim on coverage grounds?

- [ ] Yes, demanding appraisal concedes coverage
- [x] No — appraisal settles amount, not coverage
- [ ] Yes, unless it reserved rights in writing beforehand
- [ ] Only if the appraisers reach agreement

**Appraisal decides how much, never whether.** A dispute about whether the
peril is covered at all is not an appraisal question, and demanding appraisal
does not waive a coverage defence.

### After a collision the insured tells the other driver "this was entirely my fault" and hands over $800 for the bumper. What has the insured done?

- [x] Breached the duties not to admit liability and not to make voluntary payments
- [ ] Nothing improper; the payment is reimbursable as a supplementary payment
- [ ] Triggered the duty to defend early
- [ ] Waived the deductible

Liability forms forbid **admitting liability** and **making voluntary
payments**, because both interfere with the insurer's right to investigate,
defend and settle. Every demand, notice and legal paper goes to the insurer at
once instead.

### Which statement about the duty to defend is correct?

- [x] It is broader than the duty to pay and extends to groundless suits
- [ ] It applies only to suits the insurer expects to lose
- [ ] It ends when the insured's deductible is exhausted
- [ ] It is limited to the amount of the policy deductible

An insurer must defend suits that are **groundless, false or fraudulent**, so
long as the allegations fall within coverage. The duty ends when the **limit of
liability is exhausted** by judgments or settlements.

### Which form of liability coverage most commonly requires the insured's consent to settle?

- [ ] Commercial general liability
- [ ] Personal auto liability
- [x] Professional liability
- [ ] Homeowners personal liability

Most commercial and personal forms let the **insurer settle at its
discretion**. Professional liability usually requires consent, because a
settlement damages a reputation and may be reportable — and such forms often
add a **hammer clause** capping the insurer at what the refused settlement
would have cost.
