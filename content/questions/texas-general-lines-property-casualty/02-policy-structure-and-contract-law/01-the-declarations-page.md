### Which part of the policy names the mortgagee?

- [x] The declarations
- [ ] The insuring agreement
- [ ] The conditions
- [ ] The exclusions

The declarations answer **who, what, where, when and how much** — and they are
the only part unique to this insured. Everything else is printed identically
for everybody who buys the form.

### A policy period runs "1 March 2026 to 1 March 2027". Is a fire at 3 p.m. on 1 March 2027 covered?

- [ ] Yes, the final day is included
- [x] No, coverage ended at 12:01 a.m. that morning
- [ ] Yes, if the loss is reported the same day
- [ ] Only if the policy was renewed

Most policies begin and end at **12:01 a.m. standard time at the insured's
address**. The period is inclusive of its start and excludes anything after its
stated end time.

### Which document is the insurer's restatement of the application?

- [x] The declarations
- [ ] The binder
- [ ] The certificate of insurance
- [ ] The endorsement

Which is why the insured should read them on arrival: an address, a limit or a
deductible that does not match what was asked for is fixed by endorsement, not
by argument at claim time.

### An agent with binding authority binds coverage by telephone. The underwriter would have declined the risk. Is there coverage?

- [x] Yes — binding authority is the authority to obligate the insurer
- [ ] No, because the underwriter did not approve it
- [ ] Only until the insurer gives notice of rejection
- [ ] Only if the premium was paid at the time

Whether the underwriter would have chosen the risk is a matter **between agent
and insurer**, not between insurer and insured. A broker generally could not do
this; a broker represents the buyer.

### Which is true of a binder?

- [x] It may be oral, and carries the same terms as the policy it anticipates
- [ ] It must always be in writing to be effective
- [ ] It provides narrower coverage than the policy
- [ ] It continues until the insured cancels it

It is **temporary** — ending when the policy issues, when the insurer declines
and gives notice, or at its own expiry.

### A contractor's certificate of insurance names a property owner as certificate holder. A claim arises from the contractor's work. What does the owner have?

- [ ] Additional insured status
- [ ] A binder
- [x] No coverage of their own
- [ ] Loss payee rights

**A certificate creates no coverage.** It evidences that a policy existed on the
day it was issued. An owner who wants rights must be added as an **additional
insured** by endorsement.

### An endorsement excludes windstorm; the printed form lists windstorm as covered. Which governs?

- [x] The endorsement
- [ ] The printed form
- [ ] Whichever is more favourable to the insured
- [ ] Neither — the conflict voids the policy

The endorsement is the **more specific and more recent** statement of what was
agreed. (Genuine *ambiguity* between them would still be read against the
drafter, but a direct conflict is not ambiguity.)

### Which of these is NOT found on the declarations page?

- [ ] The deductible
- [ ] The forms and endorsements attached
- [ ] The policy period
- [x] The circumstances in which coverage does not apply

Those are the **exclusions**. The declarations answer who, what, where, when and
how much; the rest of the policy answers under what circumstances.

### An applicant's answers on the application are best described as what?

- [ ] Warranties
- [x] Representations — statements believed true when made
- [ ] Conditions precedent
- [ ] Binders

Representations require honesty and reasonable belief, not certainty. A
**warranty** is guaranteed true, and the consequences of breaching one are much
harsher.

### What are the four parts of any property or casualty policy?

- [ ] Application, binder, policy, endorsement
- [x] Declarations, insuring agreement, conditions, exclusions
- [ ] Declarations, premium, coverage, limits
- [ ] Insuring agreement, warranties, representations, conditions

Remembered as **DICE**, with endorsements amending any of the four. Learning
the four turns a question about an unfamiliar form into a question about which
part you are being shown.
