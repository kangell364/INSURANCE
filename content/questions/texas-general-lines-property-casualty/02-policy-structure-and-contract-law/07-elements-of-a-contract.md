### An applicant submits a completed application with the first premium. Who has made the offer?

- [x] The applicant
- [ ] The insurer, through its rate manual and brochures
- [ ] The agent, acting for the insurer
- [ ] Neither; an offer arises only when the policy is issued

**The applicant makes the offer.** The insurer accepts by **issuing the
policy**, or by **binding** coverage where the agent holds binding authority.
The brochures and rate tables are an invitation to apply, not an offer.

### An insurer issues a policy with a higher premium and an added exclusion the applicant did not request. What has it done?

- [x] Made a counter-offer, which the applicant is free to decline
- [ ] Accepted the offer, with the changes binding on the applicant
- [ ] Breached the duty of utmost good faith
- [ ] Nothing of legal significance until the premium is paid

A **counter-offer** is a rejection of the original offer plus a new one.
Different terms are not acceptance, and the applicant may walk away.

### What is the insured's consideration?

- [x] The premium and the statements made in the application
- [ ] The premium alone
- [ ] The promise to pay covered losses
- [ ] The signature on the application

Each side gives up something of value. The insured gives **premium plus
statements**; the insurer gives a **promise to pay covered losses**. The
insurer's consideration is a promise, not money, which is why an insurer that
never pays a claim has still given full consideration.

### A policy term is capable of two reasonable readings. Which is applied?

- [x] The reading favourable to the insured, because the policy is a contract of adhesion
- [ ] The reading favourable to the insurer, as drafter and risk-bearer
- [ ] Whichever a court finds the parties actually intended
- [ ] Neither; the term is struck out entirely

**Ambiguity is construed against the drafter** — *contra proferentem*. One
party wrote the words; the other took it or left it, so the cost of imprecision
falls on the one who chose the words.

### Which characteristic explains why a policy cannot be assigned to the buyer of the insured building?

- [x] Personal
- [ ] Aleatory
- [ ] Conditional
- [ ] Unilateral

The contract is with **the person, not the thing**. Selling the building does
not sell the policy, and assignment requires the insurer's consent.

### An insured stops paying premium. Can the insurer sue for breach of contract?

- [ ] Yes, the premium is an enforceable promise
- [x] No — the contract is unilateral; only the insurer made an enforceable promise
- [ ] Yes, but only for the earned portion of the term
- [ ] Only where the policy is auditable

Once the premium is paid, the insured has promised nothing further. The insurer
alone is bound to perform. The policy simply **lapses** instead.

### Which characteristic describes an exchange of unequal value depending on chance?

- [x] Aleatory
- [ ] Conditional
- [ ] Adhesion
- [ ] Utmost good faith

**Aleatory**: $1,200 in premium may buy nothing, or may buy $400,000. Buying a
car exchanges roughly equal value both ways and is not aleatory.

### An adjuster tells an insured "don't worry about the proof of loss, we have what we need". The insurer later denies the claim for lack of one. What doctrine applies?

- [x] Estoppel
- [ ] Waiver
- [ ] Rescission
- [ ] Subrogation

**Estoppel** bars a party from asserting a right because its own earlier conduct
led the other to rely on the opposite, to their detriment. **Waiver**, by
contrast, is the voluntary surrender of a known right — such as accepting a
late premium without objection.

### An applicant tells the agent about a prior fire loss; the agent leaves it off the application. Is the insurer deemed to know?

- [x] Yes — knowledge of the agent is knowledge of the insurer
- [ ] No, because the application is the insurer's only record
- [ ] Only if the applicant can prove the conversation occurred in writing
- [ ] Only where the agent had binding authority

An agent represents the **insurer**, so what the agent learns in that capacity
is imputed to the insurer. A broker represents the insured, and the same
imputation does not follow.

### A terminated agent still holds the insurer's signs, forms and rate manuals and binds a policy for a member of the public. What authority is in play?

- [x] Apparent authority
- [ ] Express authority
- [ ] Implied authority
- [ ] No authority; the binding is void

**Apparent** (ostensible) authority is what a reasonable member of the public
would believe the agent holds, from the insurer's own conduct. Leaving the
indicia of agency in place is that conduct. **Express** authority is what the
agency agreement says; **implied** is what is reasonably necessary to carry it
out.
