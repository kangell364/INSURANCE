### Mid-term, an insurer broadens its homeowners form and charges nothing extra. What do existing policyholders get?

- [x] The broader coverage automatically, under the liberalization condition
- [ ] Nothing until renewal, when the new form is issued
- [ ] The broader coverage on request and payment of a pro-rata premium
- [ ] Nothing; the form in force at inception governs the whole term

**Liberalization gives existing insureds a broadening automatically**, with no
endorsement and no extra premium, when the insurer widens the form during the
policy period without charging for it.

**The condition runs one way only.** An insurer that *narrows* a form cannot
impose that mid-term — the insured keeps what they bought. So liberalization
can only ever help the policyholder, which is why it is easy to forget it
exists.

### A fire damages a building and the insured leaves it open to the weather for a fortnight, letting rain ruin what survived. What defeats the claim for the rain damage?

- [x] The neglect exclusion — failing to save and preserve property at and after a loss
- [ ] The water damage exclusion, which removes rain
- [ ] Nothing; the rain damage flows from the covered fire
- [ ] The vacancy provision

**Neglect excludes loss caused by the insured's failure to use all reasonable
means to save and preserve the property at and after a loss.** The fire damage
is still covered; the avoidable rain damage is not.

**This is the mirror of a duty after a loss.** The conditions require the
insured to protect the property from further damage; the neglect exclusion is
what happens when they do not. One says do it, the other prices not doing it.

The second option misreads the water exclusion, which concerns surface water,
flood and sewer backup — not rain falling through a hole the insured should
have covered.

### A commercial policy's premium depends on the insured's payroll. Which condition lets the insurer verify it?

- [x] Inspection and audit
- [ ] Appraisal
- [ ] Subrogation
- [ ] Assignment

**Inspection and audit lets the insurer inspect the property and examine the
books**, which matters wherever premium is based on a variable the insured
reports — payroll for workers' compensation, sales for some general liability.

**Appraisal is the near miss and answers a different dispute.** Appraisal
resolves disagreement over the **amount of a loss**; inspection and audit is
about verifying the **exposure the premium was based on**. One is about a
claim, the other about a premium.

### Under a NAMED peril policy, a loss occurs from a cause not on the list. Who has failed to prove what?

- [x] The insured has failed to prove the loss was caused by a covered peril
- [ ] The insurer has failed to prove an exclusion applies
- [ ] Neither — the loss is covered by default
- [ ] The insured has failed to prove the amount of the loss

**Under a named peril form the burden is the insured's.** That shift of burden
is worth more to an insured than the peril list itself, and it is why an open
peril form costs more.

### Under an OPEN peril form, who must prove what?

- [ ] The insured must prove the cause is not excluded
- [x] The insurer must prove the loss falls within an exclusion
- [ ] Both parties share the burden equally
- [ ] Neither — open peril covers everything

"All risk" is a misleading name: no policy covers everything, and **the
exclusions define the coverage**.

### An insured sells an insured building and hands the policy to the buyer. Is the buyer covered?

- [ ] Yes, the policy follows the property
- [x] No — insurance is a personal contract and assignment needs the insurer's written consent
- [ ] Yes, until the next renewal
- [ ] Only if the buyer notifies the insurer within 30 days

The insurer agreed to cover **that insured**, not that building. Selling the
building does not sell the policy with it.

### A restaurant's walk-in freezer fails from age and the food spoils. Is this covered under an open peril form?

- [ ] Yes, because open peril covers all causes
- [x] No — mechanical breakdown and wear and tear are excluded as non-fortuitous
- [ ] Yes, if the spoilage exceeds the deductible
- [ ] Only if the freezer was under warranty

These are **certainties on a long enough timescale**, so they are maintenance
rather than risk. Equipment breakdown coverage is the form that answers this.

### A claims-made policy has a retroactive date of 1 January 2024. A claim is made in March 2026 for an injury in 2023. Covered?

- [ ] Yes, the claim was made during the policy period
- [x] No — the injury predates the retroactive date
- [ ] Yes, if the insured was unaware of the injury
- [ ] Only under the extended reporting period

The retroactive date bars injury before it **whenever the claim arrives**. A
retroactive date that moves forward on renewal quietly destroys years of
coverage — the thing to watch for.

### A fire destroys 60% of a building. The city requires the remainder be demolished and the replacement built to current code, adding $80,000. Which exclusion applies?

- [x] Ordinance or law
- [ ] Wear and tear
- [ ] Neglect
- [ ] Governmental action

The policy insured **the building that existed**, not the one the current code
requires. The extra cost feels like part of the fire loss and is not — which is
exactly why this catches people.

### Which best explains why flood and earthquake are excluded?

- [ ] They are always the insured's fault
- [x] The losses are correlated — one event damages every policy at once, defeating the law of large numbers
- [ ] They occur too rarely to price
- [ ] They are covered by federal law instead

Correlation is the reason. It is also why windstorm is excluded on the Texas
coast and a residual market fills the gap.

### A homeowners policy limits theft of jewellery to $1,500. A $12,000 ring is stolen. What has happened, and what is the fix?

- [x] The jewellery was limited, not excluded — $1,500 is payable, and scheduling the ring removes the sub-limit
- [ ] The jewellery was excluded — nothing is payable until it is bought back
- [ ] The full $12,000 is payable because theft is a covered peril
- [ ] Nothing is payable because the ring was not declared

**A limitation caps; an exclusion removes.** The remedies differ too: a
limitation is raised by **scheduling**, an exclusion by **buying it back**.

### An occurrence policy from 2026 faces a claim brought in 2034 for a 2026 injury. Does it respond?

- [x] Yes — an occurrence form responds to injury during the policy period, whenever the claim is made
- [ ] No, the policy period has expired
- [ ] Only if an extended reporting period was purchased
- [ ] Only if the insurer is notified within the statute of limitations

That is the defining feature of an occurrence trigger, and the reason
claims-made forms exist for long-tail exposures an insurer cannot price
forty years ahead.

### Which condition prevents an insured dumping damaged property on the insurer and demanding the limit?

- [ ] Subrogation
- [x] Abandonment
- [ ] Appraisal
- [ ] Liberalization

The **abandonment** condition. Liberalization is the opposite kind of clause
entirely — it gives existing insureds the benefit of a broadened form without
extra premium.
