### An insured drives from Houston to Monterrey. Does the personal auto policy respond to an accident there?

- [x] No — Mexico is outside the policy territory
- [ ] Yes, Mexico is included alongside Canada
- [ ] Yes, for liability but not physical damage
- [ ] Yes, for the first 30 days of the trip

The personal auto territory is the **United States, its territories and
possessions, Puerto Rico and Canada**. Mexico does not recognise foreign auto
insurance, so a Mexican policy from a Mexican insurer is required.

### A product manufactured in the United States injures a consumer in France, who sues in a French court. Is the CGL's worldwide extension likely to respond?

- [x] No — the extension generally requires the suit to be brought within the policy territory
- [ ] Yes, the products extension is unconditionally worldwide
- [ ] Yes, provided the product was manufactured in the territory
- [ ] Only if the insurer consented to the export

The worldwide reach is real but conditional: the injury may happen anywhere,
but **the lawsuit must generally be brought in the United States or Canada**.

### Who certifies an act of terrorism for TRIA purposes?

- [x] The Secretary of the Treasury
- [ ] The insurer writing the affected policy
- [ ] The state insurance commissioner
- [ ] The Department of Homeland Security

Certification is a **federal Treasury decision**, made in consultation with
other federal officials, and requires that aggregate insured losses exceed a
statutory trigger.

### A commercial insured does not want terrorism coverage. May the insurer simply leave it out of the quote?

- [x] No — the insurer must offer it, with the premium separately stated
- [ ] Yes, if the insured has said they do not want it
- [ ] Yes, for any risk outside a major metropolitan area
- [ ] No, and the insured may not decline it either

**The insurer must offer; the insured may decline.** The premium must be
separately stated so the insured knows what they are accepting or refusing, and
the rejection should be in writing.

### May a workers' compensation policy exclude terrorism?

- [x] No — statutory benefits must be paid whatever the cause
- [ ] Yes, on the same terms as any other commercial policy
- [ ] Yes, if the employer signs a written rejection
- [ ] Only where the employer operates outside a designated high-risk zone

This is the trap. **Everywhere else the insured may reject terrorism coverage;
in workers' compensation they may not**, because state law requires the policy
to cover statutory benefits regardless of cause.

### What is TRIA, structurally?

- [x] A federal reinsurance backstop that shares certified terrorism losses with insurers
- [ ] A federal insurance policy sold directly to commercial property owners
- [ ] A state residual market pool for terrorism risk
- [ ] A reserve requirement imposed on commercial insurers

Nobody buys a policy from TRIA. It is a **reinsurance programme**: each insurer
retains a deductible based on its own earned premium, above which the federal
government shares losses, subject to an annual aggregate cap on the programme.

### Which losses are generally excluded from the terrorism coverage TRIA supports?

- [x] Nuclear, biological, chemical and radiological, unless the underlying policy covers them
- [ ] Losses arising from domestic acts of terrorism
- [ ] Losses to commercial property exceeding $5 million
- [ ] Business interruption losses of any kind

NBCR losses are generally carved out. **Domestic acts have been certifiable
since the 2007 reauthorisation** removed the requirement that the act be
committed on behalf of a foreign interest.

### Are personal lines policies within the TRIA programme?

- [x] No — the programme applies to commercial lines, though personal policies are generally silent on terrorism
- [ ] Yes, homeowners policies are within it on the same terms
- [ ] Yes, but only dwelling policies on properties above $1 million
- [ ] No, and personal policies universally exclude terrorism

TRIA reaches **commercial** property and casualty. Personal forms sit outside the
programme and tend to cover terrorism by **silence**, having never excluded it.

### Under a general liability form, when does coverage extend to international waters or airspace?

- [x] While in transit between places inside the policy territory
- [ ] At all times, without restriction
- [ ] Only for vessels and aircraft the insured owns
- [ ] Never; water and air are excluded

The extension is for **transit**, not for a destination — a shipment moving
between two points in the territory does not fall out of coverage mid-ocean.

### Where does a property form cover property genuinely in transit?

- [x] Under an inland marine form, not the property form's premises coverage
- [ ] Automatically, anywhere in the world
- [ ] Under the territory provision, up to the policy limit
- [ ] Under the terrorism endorsement

Property coverage **attaches to described premises**. Property away from them is
covered only by a specific extension, and property genuinely in transit belongs
on an **inland marine** form.
