### A $60,000 loss is covered by two pro rata policies with limits of $200,000 and $100,000. What does the larger policy pay?

- [x] $40,000
- [ ] $60,000
- [ ] $30,000
- [ ] $20,000

Each insurer pays the share **its limit bears to the total of all limits**:
200/300 × $60,000 = $40,000, and 100/300 × $60,000 = $20,000.

### On those same facts, what does the insured receive in total?

- [x] $60,000
- [ ] $120,000
- [ ] $40,000
- [ ] $30,000

The insured collects **the loss, once**. Adding the two payments together and
answering $120,000 is the trap this question exists to set; indemnity restores,
it does not enrich.

### After a fire caused by a contractor's negligence, the insured signs a release of the contractor and then claims on the policy. What is the likely result?

- [x] Coverage is void for that loss — the insured destroyed the subrogation right
- [ ] Coverage responds in full; subrogation is the insurer's problem
- [ ] Coverage responds, reduced by what the insurer could have recovered
- [ ] Coverage responds only if the release was signed under duress

A waiver signed **after** a loss prejudices the insurer's recovery and can void
coverage. A waiver signed **before** a loss — a hold harmless in a lease or
construction contract — is usually permitted. The before-and-after distinction
is heavily examined.

### A friend borrows the insured's car and causes an accident. Which policy is primary?

- [x] The car owner's
- [ ] The driver's own policy
- [ ] Whichever has the higher limit
- [ ] Both share by equal shares

**Auto liability follows the car.** The owner's policy is primary; the
permissive driver's own policy sits excess above it.

### A liability policy has a $300,000 limit. The insurer spends $75,000 defending a suit that settles for $300,000. What is the total outlay?

- [x] $375,000
- [ ] $300,000
- [ ] $225,000
- [ ] $75,000

Defence costs are **supplementary payments** — paid in addition to the limit.

### On those same facts, a second covered suit arrives the following week. What does the insurer owe?

- [x] Nothing — the limit is exhausted and the duty to defend has ended
- [ ] A full defence, because each suit carries its own defence obligation
- [ ] Defence costs only, with no indemnity
- [ ] Half of the original limit

Supplementary payments and the duty to defend both **stop when the limit is
exhausted** by payment of judgments or settlements.

### An insurer pays a $10,000 claim on which the insured bore a $1,000 deductible, then recovers $6,000 from the party at fault. Who gets what?

- [x] The insured receives $1,000 and the insurer keeps $5,000
- [ ] The insurer keeps the entire $6,000
- [ ] The insured receives $6,000
- [ ] The recovery is split equally

The insured is generally **made whole first**: recovered money goes to the
insured's uninsured loss, deductible included, before the insurer recoups.

### Two liability policies with limits of $100,000 and $50,000 share a $90,000 loss by contribution by equal shares. What does each pay?

- [x] $45,000 each
- [ ] $60,000 and $30,000
- [ ] $50,000 and $40,000
- [ ] $90,000 and nothing

Under **equal shares**, each insurer pays the same amount until the loss is paid
or its limit is exhausted. Had the loss been $120,000, the smaller policy would
stop at $50,000 and the larger would pay the remaining $70,000.

### A court requires an appeal bond of $100,000. What does the liability insurer pay as a supplementary payment?

- [x] The premium for the bond, not its face amount
- [ ] The full $100,000 face amount
- [ ] Half the face amount
- [ ] Nothing; appeal bonds are the insured's expense

The insurer pays **bond premiums**, not bond amounts. The same applies to bail
bonds arising from a covered accident, subject to a stated maximum — commonly
$250 in auto forms.

### To which of these does subrogation NOT apply?

- [x] Life insurance
- [ ] Homeowners property coverage
- [ ] Commercial general liability
- [ ] Personal auto physical damage

Subrogation belongs to **contracts of indemnity** — property and liability.
Life insurance is a **valued contract**: there is no over-recovery to prevent
and no recovery in the indemnity sense to pursue.
