### An underwriter orders a report that will involve interviewing the applicant's neighbours. What kind of report is it?

- [x] An investigative consumer report
- [ ] A consumer report
- [ ] An inspection report
- [ ] An agent's report

The **personal interview** is the entire distinction. A **consumer report**
draws on records and databases; an **investigative consumer report** gathers
information by interviewing neighbours, friends, associates and employers.

### What must the applicant be told when an investigative consumer report may be ordered?

- [x] In writing, generally within three days, and that they may request its nature and scope
- [ ] Nothing, provided the report is used only for underwriting
- [ ] Orally at the time of application, with no written follow-up
- [ ] Only if the report results in a declination

The extra intrusion brings an extra duty. Notice is **in writing within about
three days of the request**, and it must tell the applicant they may ask for a
complete disclosure of the report's nature and scope.

### An insurer charges a higher premium because of a credit-based insurance score. What must it tell the applicant?

- [x] That adverse action was taken, the reporting agency's name, address and phone, and that the agency did not make the decision
- [ ] Only the name of the reporting agency
- [ ] The numerical score and the factors that produced it
- [ ] Nothing, because the policy was issued rather than declined

Charging more **is** an adverse action, as are declining, cancelling and
non-renewing. The insurer must identify the source and make clear the agency
neither made the decision nor can explain it.

### The applicant believes the consumer report contains an error. Whom do they contact?

- [x] The consumer reporting agency, which must reinvestigate
- [ ] The insurer, which must correct its underwriting file
- [ ] The state insurance department
- [ ] The agent who submitted the application

**The agency — not the insurer — must fix it.** The agency reinvestigates,
generally within 30 days, and corrects or deletes what it cannot verify. If the
consumer still disagrees, they may file a **statement of dispute** that
accompanies the file.

### How long may most adverse information remain on a consumer report?

- [x] Seven years, and ten for a bankruptcy
- [ ] Three years, and seven for a bankruptcy
- [ ] Ten years for everything
- [ ] Indefinitely, until the consumer disputes it

Seven years is the general rule; **bankruptcy is the ten-year exception**.

### An insurer wishes to share customer information with an unaffiliated marketing company. What must it do first?

- [x] Provide a privacy notice and a reasonable opportunity to opt out
- [ ] Obtain the customer's written opt-in consent
- [ ] Notify the state insurance commissioner
- [ ] Nothing; disclosure to third parties is unrestricted

GLBA is built on **opt-out, not opt-in** — sharing is permitted unless the
consumer objects. No opt-out is required for disclosures needed to service the
account, process a transaction, or comply with law.

### Which law is concerned with the accuracy of consumer reports, and which with the privacy of non-public personal information?

- [x] FCRA for accuracy, GLBA for privacy
- [ ] GLBA for accuracy, FCRA for privacy
- [ ] HIPAA for accuracy, FCRA for privacy
- [ ] Both are governed by GLBA

**FCRA**: accuracy and fairness, triggered by adverse action, remedied by a free
copy and a dispute. **GLBA**: privacy, triggered by the relationship itself,
addressed by the privacy notice and the opt-out.

### When must a GLBA privacy notice be given?

- [x] When the relationship is established, and annually thereafter
- [ ] Only once, at the time of application
- [ ] Only when the insurer intends to share information
- [ ] At each renewal and at each claim

The notice must state **what is collected, what is disclosed and to whom** —
initially and then annually.

### A property claim database shows a claim made by the previous owner of a house. Why can that affect the new buyer's premium?

- [x] Loss history databases record claims against the address as well as the person
- [ ] The new buyer inherits the previous owner's policy record
- [ ] The FCRA requires prior claims to be attributed to the current occupant
- [ ] It cannot; only the applicant's own claims may be considered

In property, the **address carries a loss history**. It is legitimate
underwriting information, and a buyer who has never made a claim can still face
a higher premium because of one.

### Which GLBA obligation requires administrative, technical and physical protection of customer information?

- [x] The safeguards rule
- [ ] The privacy notice
- [ ] The opt-out
- [ ] The pretexting prohibition

The **safeguards rule** is the security obligation. **Pretexting** — obtaining
somebody's financial information under false pretences — is separately
prohibited.
